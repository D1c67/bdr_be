Story (design "Scheduling and proactivity", `docs/ASSISTANT.md:140`; updated for the 2026-10-30 owner decisions): at 7:00 AM Pacific an Estimating Engineer (Materials) gets one email, "Your assistant digest", and in BDR sees a dot on the assistant launcher and one bell row, "Your 7:00 AM digest: 3 items". The email and the Inbox tab on /assistant show the same lines: bid 26.10.7301 is due tomorrow and Gear and Lighting quotes are still missing (his part); a to-do he owns is due today; and "4 other updates reached your bell since yesterday 1:00 PM". At 2:15 PM a vendor quote for a bid that was already submitted is extracted by the RFQ poller; BDR itself now bells it as a late quote for both engineering focuses (the `rfq_inbox.py` fix, 4.5), so the router only records it quietly in his Inbox, collapsed by the email's message ID with the "vendor replied" bell from the same email, and sends nothing. At 4:00 PM an assistant approval card he created at 1:00 PM is an hour from expiring: the router rings one bell and sends him one Teams message. He marks the to-do item "Not useful" three times over the next two weeks; the router moves to-do reminders to inbox-only for him and says so in the next digest. At 7:40 PM a second approval card nears expiry: it shows in the app at once, and its Teams message waits for 6:00 AM (and is skipped if he has already read it or the card is gone). Nothing in this story re-announces a fact BDR's own notifications already delivered, and record rows never go to Teams or email.

Phase 7 in one paragraph: a deterministic, model-free router turns facts into per-user inbox rows. Facts enter `assistant_events` (the 0170 outbox) from three emitters that need no edit to any domain file: a tail of the `notifications` table (every bell a user already got becomes a "record" row, covered by 0177; this now includes the AI-path `late_quote.received` bell that the `rfq_inbox.py` fix adds, recorded as `bell.late_quote`), a tail of `audit_log` (failed to bid, which today is email only), and a sweeper (bids due soon whose owner's part is missing, to-dos due, assistant approval cards about to expire). The one domain edit of the phase is that separate `rfq_inbox.py` fix (4.5, WI-10). A claim RPC lets both uvicorn workers route events into `assistant_inbox` (UNIQUE(user_id, dedupe_key), one row per user, never assistant-to-assistant). Severity decides delivery: high items ring one in-app bell and send one Teams message immediately outside quiet hours (6 PM to 6 AM PT), urgent items do so at any hour; normal items wait for the 7:00 AM and 1:00 PM PT workday digests, each delivered as one Inbox digest, one bell and one email; covered facts are recorded and never pushed, never sent to Teams or email. Every external send goes through the router's own ledger (`assistant_external_sends`, unique on target and channel, claim, lease, retry, dead letter), reusing the existing Teams and Graph email helpers and their kill switches. A precedence table against due_reminders, calling_in, auto_send, the 0177 routed bells and the phase 3 recipes decides suppress, record, merge or push for every pair; the 6:03 due_digest is independent of the router (owner question 8). Feedback buttons (useful, not useful, mute this kind, mute this project, remind me next digest) change routing deterministically through per-user `assistant_subscriptions` overrides. 0177 keeps owning BDR's own email and Teams unchanged; the router never re-sends an app notification. No caps on bells or digest length (owner question 16).

1. `assistant_events_claim(p_worker text, p_limit int, p_lease_seconds int, p_now timestamptz default now(), p_ids uuid[] default null) returns setof assistant_events`, plpgsql, two statements in one transaction.
   - First, retire poison events: `update assistant_events set status = 'failed', processed_at = p_now, last_error = 'lease_exhausted' where processed_at is null and status = 'claimed' and claimed_at < p_now - make_interval(secs => greatest(60, p_lease_seconds)) and attempts >= 5 and (p_ids is null or id = any(p_ids))`. An event that kills or hangs its worker five times is dead-lettered here, not handed out again.
   - Then claim, in the `claim_due_assistant_schedules` shape (`0176:195-240`) and the batch shape of `assistant_browser_notice_claim` (`0187:130-137`): `with picked as (select id from assistant_events where processed_at is null and attempts < 5 and (p_ids is null or id = any(p_ids)) and (next_attempt_at is null or next_attempt_at <= p_now) and (status = 'pending' or (status = 'claimed' and claimed_at < p_now - make_interval(secs => greatest(60, p_lease_seconds)))) order by occurred_at, id limit greatest(1, least(p_limit, 200)) for update skip locked) update assistant_events e set status = 'claimed', claimed_by = p_worker, claimed_at = p_now, attempts = e.attempts + 1 from picked where e.id = picked.id returning e.*`. ## 4. Emitters

### 4.1 Shape

All emitters live in `app/services/assistant_proactive.py` (WI-5) and write `assistant_events` through repo system-sweep functions (`repo.insert_events(None, rows)` upserting `on_conflict=dedupe_key` with ignore-duplicates; `assistant_events_dedupe_key_key` is a full unique constraint, so PostgREST can target it). Every event carries `source`, `event_type`, `dedupe_key` (always `p7:` plus the type), `project_id`, `subject_user_id` (for one-person facts), `occurred_at` (the source row's time) and a payload validated against the type's allowlist in `proactive.py` (unknown keys dropped and logged, 4 KB cap, no strings longer than 64 characters except ISO times). A public `emit()` helper is not built in phase 7 (no domain call sites, owner question 18).

Tails share one keyset routine. There are two tails (the quotes tail was dropped on 2026-10-30, owner question 17). Each tail names its clock column, pinned in the service test: `notifications.created_at`, `audit_log.created_at`. Both default to transaction start, and both ids are uuids (dev `information_schema.columns`).

1. Read the cursor row `(cursor_at, cursor_id)`.
2. Compute the upper bound `ub = now`.
3. Select source rows with `(clock, id) > (cursor_at - 2 minutes, cursor_id or the nil uuid)` and `clock <= ub`, ordered by `(clock, id)`, limit 500. The 2-minute overlap is for transactions that commit late.
4. Build events and insert them (ignore duplicates).
5. Advance the cursor with a fenced update (`cursor_at = <old> and cursor_id is not distinct from <old>`):
   - a full page moves it to the last `(clock, id)` read, which is strictly past the overlap start, so a page full of rows inside the overlap can no longer pin the cursor (no livelock);
   - a short page, including an empty one, moves it to `least(now - 2 minutes, ub)` with `cursor_id = null`, so quiet tails (failed-to-bid) never age.
   - A lost fence means the other worker advanced it, and the dedupe keys make the overlap harmless.

Lag guard (replaces the earlier "24 hour jump"): at loop start, a cursor older than `ASSISTANT_PROACTIVE_CURSOR_MAX_LAG_MINUTES` (default 60) is reseeded to `now - 2 minutes` and the step logs `gap_skipped` with the count of rows actually skipped (one `count(*)` over the skipped range, logged only when it is above zero). The same reseed runs when the company switch flips from off to on (10.1). A Railway deploy restart (minutes) replays nothing and loses nothing; a long outage or the first start after a release does not replay a day of bells. The tails keep running and advancing while the DB company switch is off (6.1), so turning it back on replays nothing either.

The tails are cheap: `notifications_created_at_idx` (A9), `audit_log_action_idx (action, created_at)` (dev pg_indexes).

### 4.3 Placement, sync and loss

- All emitters run inside the router loop thread (`asyncio.to_thread(poll_once)`), plain `def`, never in a request handler. No request path gains latency or a failure mode.
- Loss: a tail never loses a committed row while the loop runs (keyset cursor, overlap plus dedupe). After a stop longer than `ASSISTANT_PROACTIVE_CURSOR_MAX_LAG_MINUTES`, rows from the gap are skipped on purpose and counted in the `gap_skipped` log. The sweeper recomputes from state at each slot, so a missed slot only means the next slot shows current state.
- Exactly once per user: the event `dedupe_key` (global unique, `assistant_events_dedupe_key_key`) and the inbox `UNIQUE(user_id, dedupe_key)` (`assistant_inbox_dedupe_uq`).

## 5. Sweeper

### 5.1 Cadence

- Every tick (`ASSISTANT_PROACTIVE_POLL_SECONDS`, default 30): the two tails, the approval-expiring scan, routing (section 6), delivery of every due immediate row (8.4, which also covers rows a crashed worker left undelivered), digest delivery for open slots (8.2), then the send step that claims and settles due `assistant_external_sends` rows (8.5).
- Per slot (07:00 and 13:00 PT on digest days, within the 3 hour catch-up window): `bid.due_not_ready` and `todo.due`, run by the one worker that claims `assistant_proactive_slots (digest_am | digest_pm, slot_date)`.
- Once per Pacific day after 02:00 PT: the purge (`purge` slot, B4, send ledger included) and project-closed retirement of mutes.

### 5.2 Slot claim and lease

`repo.claim_proactive_slot(None, step, slot_date, worker, now)` inserts the ledger row with `claimed_at = now` (a duplicate key means another worker holds it). A row with `finished_at is null` and `claimed_at` older than 15 minutes (longer than the 600 s event lease, so the dead holder's slot events are reclaimable by then) is reclaimed by a fenced update (`claimed_at = <old>`), which also bumps `result.attempts`.

The holder:

1. Builds the slot's events and inserts them already claimed (`status = 'claimed'`, `claimed_by = holder`, `claimed_at = now`, `attempts = 1`), so the other worker's general claim (`p_ids = null`) skips them while the lease runs (A11.1).
2. Looks up the slot's event ids by their dedupe keys (this covers events a dead holder inserted earlier) and claims any still pending or stale through `assistant_events_claim(..., p_ids => ids)`.
3. Routes them in the same tick (6.2 for just those events).
4. Sets `finished_at` and a counts-only `result` only after every slot event is `processed`, `skipped` or `failed`. An event re-queued with a retry backoff keeps the slot open until it settles.

Digest delivery for that slot waits for `finished_at` (8.2), so a digest never goes out before its due-soon lines exist.

A failing sweeper does not block the digest. Each failed attempt (an exception in the emit or route step) increments `result.failures` and records `result.error` (the exception class only). After 2 failed attempts, or once local time passes the slot instant plus 90 minutes (inside the 3 hour catch-up window), the holder sets `finished_at` with `result.error`, and the digest goes out without that slot's sweeper lines. Held high items and snoozed rows still go. The counts-only health block (9.3) shows the failure.

### 5.3 Due soon and not ready (the queries)

At slot time `t` (Pacific date `d`): the window is `[t, end of the next digest day)`, where "next digest day" skips weekends and holidays, so a Friday 7 AM slot covers Friday and Monday bids. Membership is `due_digest.due_today_projects(sb, start, end)` with the window bounds (it filters `current_stage not in EXCLUDED_STAGES` and `abandoned_at is null`, `due_digest.py:68`, `:278-291`), then `readiness_rows` for checks (booleans only, 4.4) and WI-5's own `rfqs` query (`project_id, material_category_id`, no names) for the quote part. A project with no RFQ rows has no quote part (`quotes_missing = false`), matching `due_reminders.missing_quote_categories` returning None for it. One event per project per slot; the router narrows to roles whose part is missing. Projects whose `internal_bid_at` already passed are left out (the due_reminders `expired` bell covers them).

### 5.4 Idempotency

Slot events carry the slot in the key, so a reclaimed slot re-emits the same keys (no duplicates). The approval scan's key includes `expires_at`, so an extended card can warn again. To-do keys include the due value, so moving a due date re-arms. The purge is bounded and repeatable.

## 6. Router

### 6.1 Loop and kill switches

`app/services/assistant_proactive.py`: `WORKER_ID = f"{socket.gethostname()}:{os.getpid()}:{uuid4().hex[:8]}"` (as `assistant_scheduler.py:44`), plain `def poll_once(now: datetime | None = None, *, worker: str | None = None, tails: bool = True) -> dict` (`worker` defaults to `WORKER_ID`; `tails=False` is for the dev tick script only, 11.5; both are fixed in WI-1's stub signature) with each step in its own try (one step's failure never stops the others), `async def polling_loop()` in the guarded shape. Started in `main.py` after the assistant_calendar block (`app/main.py:135-139`) when `settings.assistant_enabled and settings.assistant_proactive_enabled and settings.supabase_url`, and added to the shutdown tuple (`app/main.py:331-335`). Independent of `ASSISTANT_SCHEDULER_ENABLED` (the maintenance tick is coupled to the scheduler, `assistant_scheduler.py:814-820`, so a rider there would have no own switch). Each tick first reads `assistant_settings` (`repo.settings_row(None)`; WI-3 adds `proactive_enabled` to `SETTINGS_COLUMNS`, `app/assistant/repo.py:78`, which today selects `id, enabled, model, global_daily_cap_usd, disabled_tools, holidays, updated_at` only, and pins it in `tests/test_assistant_repo_phase7.py`; no existing test pins that string). When `enabled` or `proactive_enabled` is false:
- the two tails keep running and advancing their cursors;
- every claimed event, including the ones the tails just wrote, is finished `skipped` with `routed = {"skipped": "switched_off"}`;
- no slot sweeper runs and nothing is delivered;
- the send step still claims due `assistant_external_sends` rows but settles each `skipped` with error kind `channel_off`, so nothing queued before the switch went off is sent later (no replay);
- undelivered inbox rows stay undelivered, and the delivery recheck (6.4) applies when the switch returns.

Turning the switch back on therefore replays nothing. The allowlist pilot (12.2) runs with the switch on and the allowlist set, never with the switch off.

### 6.2 Routing one event (deterministic)

For each claimed event (`assistant_events_claim(worker, ASSISTANT_PROACTIVE_CLAIM_BATCH, 600)`):

1. Catalog lookup (`proactive.EVENT_TYPES`). Unknown type: finish `failed` (`unknown_type`).
2. Candidate recipients: `subject_user_id` for one-person kinds and `bell.*`; otherwise every active profile whose role is in the kind's audience (catalog default roles plus admin role rows with `enabled = true`, minus admin role rows with `enabled = false`). `bell.*` audiences are also bounded by the 0177 event audience (`Event.audience`); the router never widens who hears a fact.
3. Live-test allowlist: when `ASSISTANT_PROACTIVE_USER_ALLOWLIST` is set, keep only listed users.
4. Eligibility per user (bulk: one profiles read, one `assistant_profiles` read for the set, the settings row once): `assistant_enabled_for` (`access.py:63-90`) and `features.enabled_map(role)[sub_app]` for the kind's sub-app (`app/core/features.py`). No delegation test (D7). A missing `assistant_profiles` row counts as enabled, as `assistant_enabled_for` already treats it (the row is created lazily; `bdr.e2e.admin@g3test.dev` has none on dev). Ineligible users get nothing and count in `routed.ineligible`.
5. Subscription per user: user row (enabled, severity) over admin role row over catalog default. `enabled = false` drops the user (`routed.muted`). Project mute (`assistant_project_mutes`) drops router-owned and record rows for that project (`routed.project_muted`).
6. Role narrowing for `bid.due_not_ready`: keep a user only if a part their role owns is missing (5.3); Executive and IT Admin (owner question 30, a) count as owning every part.
7. Precedence (section 7) per (user, event): `push`, `record`, `merge`, `suppress` or `note`. `suppress` writes nothing; the reason counts in `routed`.
8. Build the row with `proactive.render(event, recipient_role, facts)`: title, why, payload (ids plus server labels), `severity`, `delivery` (`record` for record kinds and `low`; `digest` for normal; `immediate` for high and urgent), `precedence` (the rule id), `event_id`, `event_type`, `project_id`, `created_at = now`. `deliver_after` is always set on router-owned rows:
   - immediate: `now`, or the next 06:00 PT when held by quiet hours (8.4);
   - digest: `proactive.next_slot(now)`, the slot instant the row belongs to (no 6:03 hold: the router never reads `due_digest_log`, owner question 8);
   - record rows: `source_notification_id` = the bell id, `delivered_at = now` (delivered by definition), no `deliver_after`.
9. Upsert into `assistant_inbox` with `on_conflict=user_id,dedupe_key` ignore-duplicates (full unique constraint). Record rows with an `email_key` (4.2a) go through `assistant_inbox_link_bell` (A11.11) instead, so a second bell from the same email links to the first row. For `bid.due_not_ready`, the same step dismisses with reason `superseded` any older undelivered or snoozed `bid.due_not_ready` row for the same (user, project) (one repo call after the insert), so a rolled or snoozed line never sits next to the fresh one in a digest.
10. Delivery depends on row state, not on whether this pass inserted the row. After the upsert, the router re-selects the event's undelivered immediate rows by (user_id, dedupe_key) with `deliver_after <= now`, and calls `assistant_inbox_deliver(user, id, bell_message, metadata, teams, teams_not_before, now)` for each (A11.3, no cap). `teams` is true when the row's severity is high or urgent, `ASSISTANT_PROACTIVE_EXTERNAL_ENABLED` is on, and the person's Teams switch is not off (`teams_alert_users.enabled is false` means off; a missing row means on, `teams_alerts.py:157`, `:525-528`, so the first send calls `_deliver`, which may install BDR Alerts and creates the person's row; owner questions 35 and 36, answered 2026-10-30: a missing row is treated as on, a switched-off row means in-app only, and the router follows only the switches that exist, P35, P36). `teams_not_before` is the next 06:00 PT only for a non-urgent `assistant.approval_expiring` row delivered inside quiet hours (P31); an urgent one gets its Teams ledger row at once (P39). The per-tick release step (8.4) delivers every immediate row with `delivered_at is null and dismissed_at is null and deliver_after <= now`. So a row inserted by a worker that died, lost its lease or raised before delivery is delivered on a later tick by either worker, and the RPC's fence prevents a second bell and a second Teams ledger row.
11. `assistant_event_finish(processed, routed counts)`. Counts only, never names.

Never assistant-to-assistant: every inbox row is written by the router for exactly one `user_id`; no code path reads one user's assistant rows to write another user's; the fan-out is the router's, as a system actor. The bell rows carry `metadata.actor_kind = 'system'` (the reserved kind, `docs/ASSISTANT.md:489`).

### 6.4 Failures

- A routing exception on one event: `assistant_event_finish(retry, error_kind)` with backoff 60 s, 5 min, 30 min, 2 h; at 5 attempts `failed` (dead letter, visible in a counts-only admin view and the build log). Transport failures on the claim or the tails log and end the step.
- Lease 600 s: an event whose worker died is reclaimed; `attempts` already counted it. An event that kills or hangs its worker five times is failed `lease_exhausted` inside the claim RPC (A11.1), so a poison event cannot take a batch slot forever.
- A worker that dies between the inbox insert and the delivery RPC leaves an undelivered row with `deliver_after` set; the per-tick release step delivers it (6.2 step 10). FakeDB test: crash after the insert, reclaim, exactly one delivery and one bell.
- A lost delivery fence (`delivered: false`) is normal under two workers and is not an error.
- External send failures are the send ledger's, not the event's (8.5): an event is `processed` once its inbox rows are written, and a Teams or email failure never re-routes the event or re-rings the bell.
- Delivery-time recheck: before `assistant_inbox_deliver` and before attaching rows to a digest, the router rechecks eligibility and subscription for the user's current role; rows that fail are dismissed `superseded`. Rows for projects now closed (abandoned, declined, failed to bid, `bid_outcome`, `pm_only`, `cp_only`; for `bid.due_not_ready` also `submitted`) are dismissed `project_closed`. A `bid.due_not_ready` row whose payload `internal_bid_at` is at or before now, or differs from the project's current `internal_bid_at`, is dismissed `superseded`, so a snoozed or rolled "due tomorrow" line is never delivered after the deadline or for a moved date. A record row whose source bell has `dismissed_at` set is hidden from lists and counts (BDR withdrew the bell when the task was done, for example `due.*` at send_out, `workflow.py:301-330`, or `call_in` on close, `calling_in.py:1181-1190`).

### 6.5 Both uvicorn workers

Correctness lives in Postgres: the claim RPC (`for update skip locked`, lease), cursor fences, the slot ledger, the inbox unique constraint, the digest ledger unique constraint, the delivered fence, and for external sends the `assistant_external_sends_target_uq` constraint plus the send claim and settle fences (A11.12, A11.13). No leader election, no process-local state beyond monotonic throttles.

### 6.8 Offboarding

## 8. Digests and quiet hours

### 8.1 Slots and days

Slots are 07:00 (`am`) and 13:00 (`pm`) Pacific wall time (`ASSISTANT_PROACTIVE_DIGEST_TIMES`), on Monday to Friday excluding `assistant_settings.holidays` (P6; the same list routines use, `assistant_scheduler._holidays`, `:140`). The instant is `datetime.combine(d, time(7), tzinfo=PT)`; neither slot falls in the 02:00 to 03:00 DST gap or the repeated 01:00 hour, so no fold handling is needed, but the pure helper `proactive.slot_instant(d, slot)` is tested on 2026-11-01, 2026-11-02, 2027-03-14 and 2027-03-15. The catch-up window is `catchup_hours`, 3 hours by default (07:00 to 10:00, 13:00 to 16:00); the 12.1 validator keeps every slot plus its window outside quiet hours. A slot not delivered inside its window is skipped and its pending rows wait for the next slot, where the supersede rule (6.2 step 9) and the delivery recheck (6.4) drop stale or duplicate `bid.due_not_ready` lines.

### 8.2 Content and delivery

A digest groups, for one user: overnight high items held by quiet hours (top, marked high), `bid.due_not_ready` lines (one per project, the user's part only), `todo.due` lines, snoozed rows returning, and one line "N other updates reached your bell since <since_at, Pacific>" counted from record rows whose source bell is still unread and undismissed. Record rows are never listed one by one (owner question 1, decision I). One rule sets the window: `since_at` = the later of the user's last delivered digest's `created_at` and now minus 3 days, computed in the RPC (A11.4), stored on the ledger row and used for the label, so an empty Friday PM, a missed Monday AM or a holiday never misstates it. It lists every attached item (no render limit, owner question 16), highest severity first, then oldest. Delivery is `assistant_digest_deliver` (A11.4): ledger claim, attach, one `assistant.digest` bell and, when external sends are on, one email ledger row (8.5). An empty slot writes nothing and sends nothing (the phase 3 rule that an empty run rings no bell, `docs/ASSISTANT_PHASE3_PLAN.md:1599` question 5). The "next slot" for snooze is `proactive.next_slot(now)`.

The digest email (8.5) shows exactly the Inbox digest: the same rendered titles and whys in the same order, the "other updates" count line, the digest's Pacific slot label and a link to `/assistant?tab=inbox&digest=<digest_id>`. Ids, enums, counts, Pacific times and project numbers only; no free text, no amounts (decision A).

Per tick while a slot is open and its `assistant_proactive_slots` row has `finished_at`: select distinct `user_id` from pending digest rows (`assistant_inbox_pending_digest_idx`) with `deliver_after <= <slot instant>` (the same predicate as the RPC, A11.4; `deliver_after` is never null on a digest row, enforced by `assistant_inbox_delivery_chk`), skip users who already have a ledger row for this slot, recheck each user (6.4), call the RPC per user. A row that becomes due after the user's digest went out (a new normal item, a returning snooze) carries the next slot's instant and is not polled again; a user with no AM digest whose first normal item arrives at 08:30 gets it at 13:00, not a late "7:00 AM digest". Users in quiet hours do not exist at 07:00 or 13:00, so the slot check is enough.

The router's digest email is its own send (8.5) and does not use `due_digest`'s ledger or its claim-rollback pattern (`due_digest.py:431-441`).

### 8.4 Quiet hours

The window is 18:00 to 06:00 PT every day (`ASSISTANT_PROACTIVE_QUIET_START`, `_END`), fixed company hours shown read-only (owner question 4). It applies to the router only, including its Teams and email (owner question 3); BDR's existing email, Teams and due reminders are unchanged. Held: high immediate rows created inside the window get `deliver_after = the next 06:00 PT`; rows that can go at once get `deliver_after = now` (6.2 step 8). Exceptions: urgent rows and `assistant.approval_expiring` rows get `deliver_after = now` at any hour (questions 5 and 33). The per-tick step `release_held` selects every row with `delivery = 'immediate' and delivered_at is null and dismissed_at is null and deliver_after <= now` (`assistant_inbox_pending_immediate_idx`), held or not, so it also delivers rows a crashed worker left behind.

"Held" needs no new column (review finding quiet-hours-ids-7, 15.3): a row is held when its severity is high, its `event_type` is not `assistant.approval_expiring`, and `deliver_after > created_at`. Only the quiet-hours hold sets `deliver_after` later than the insert instant on an immediate row, so a crash leftover (`deliver_after = created_at`), an urgent row and an approval-expiring row are never held and never fold. For each selected row:
- a held row goes through `assistant_inbox_fold` (A11.14), which runs under the same per-user lock as the digest claim (A11.4), so the "AM digest not delivered" check and the flip are atomic with it. On a digest day, before `am_slot + catchup_hours` (10:00 with the defaults) and while the user has no AM digest ledger row, the row folds into the AM digest (`delivery = 'digest'`, precedence `quiet_hours`, `deliver_after` = the AM slot instant). It then reaches the person in the 7:00 AM digest email and gets no Teams message of its own (owner question 5, option a). Otherwise A11.14 answers `deliver`;
- every other row, and every held row A11.14 answers `deliver` for, is delivered at once through `assistant_inbox_deliver`, with its Teams message (A11.3). That covers weekends and holidays, where held high items are sent alone at 06:00 PT by Teams and there is no digest (question 6), a release at 10:30 after a server outage, and a user whose AM digest already went out.
- Un-fold (review finding quiet-hours-ids-1): the same step also selects folded rows (`precedence = 'quiet_hours'`, `delivery = 'digest'`, `delivered_at is null`, `dismissed_at is null`) and passes each to A11.14. When the user's AM digest went out without the row, or the AM window closed with no AM digest (an outage, or a slot held open by sweeper retries), the row goes back to `delivery = 'immediate'`, `deliver_after = now`, and is delivered at once through A11.3 with its Teams message (decision A: "otherwise it is sent at once, by Teams"). A folded row never waits for the 13:00 digest.

So a "high" item never waits until 13:00 or the next workday after quiet hours ended. On workdays an overnight item waits for the 07:00 digest email while an item created at 06:01 rings and goes to Teams at once (question 5, option a, as written). Urgent rows ignore the window: bell and Teams at any hour, quiet hours included (question 5). `assistant.approval_expiring` shows in the app (Inbox row and bell) during quiet hours, and at the default high severity its Teams ledger row carries `not_before` = the next 06:00 PT (question 33). When an admin has set the kind to urgent for the person's role, the row is urgent and its Teams message goes at once even in quiet hours, an explicit exception to question 33 (owner question 39, b). For a high row, at 06:00 the send step skips it (`stale`) when the card is no longer pending or the person has already read or dismissed the item in the app. (Guiding-principle call: a Teams message about something the person already saw in the app, or about a card that no longer exists, would only repeat what the app showed.) Normal rows are unaffected (slots are outside the window). Record rows are written at any hour (they push nothing and never go external). Quiet hours never gate 0177 bells, email or Teams (P3), nor due_reminders, calling_in or auto_send. Quiet hours also gate the send step itself (8.5 step 4), so a retry, a test-session hold or a backlog never sends a non-urgent Teams message or a digest email inside the window. Boundary tests: 17:59:59 and 18:00:00, 05:59:59 and 06:00:00, the DST change nights, an urgent row at 23:00 (bell and Teams at once), an approval warning at 18:30 (bell at once, Teams at 06:00), an urgent approval warning at 23:00 (bell and Teams at once) and a high one at 23:00 (bell at once, Teams at 06:00), a Saturday 06:00 release (Teams, no digest).

## 13. Risks

1. Record-row volume: every allowlisted bell becomes a row (194 non-noise bells in 30 days on dev; prod is busier). Mitigation: 90-day purge, the allowlist (P19); the owner kept record rows as the Phase 8 triage feed (question 1), and emptying the allowlist still turns them off without code changes (a config tuple in `proactive.py`, test-pinned).
2. The bell tail depends on `notifications.created_at` (default `now()`, transaction start). A producer that inserts with an explicit older `created_at`, or a transaction open longer than 2 minutes, is missed. Today no producer sets it (grep in WI-5 confirms before build); the overlap is configurable.
3. The late AI quote now depends on the `rfq_inbox.py` fix (4.5) landing; until it does, an AI-extracted late quote still rings only the plain `quote.received` bell and the router records it as `bell.quote_received` with no "late" wording (the 2026-10-29 router push is dropped). Mitigation: WI-10 is independent of 0190 and can ship first; it is built inside Phase 7 (owner question 37, a). A quote that arrives while a proposal revision is open is not late by design (`workflow.py:580-593`).
4. Duplicate or lost external sends. Neither `graph_email.send_mail` nor `teams_alerts._deliver` is idempotent, so the ledger retries only when nothing can have left and retires a stale claim or an ambiguous timeout as `uncertain` (8.5 step 6, A11.12). Residual risk: a rare missed Teams message or digest email, never a duplicate; the bell and Inbox row always exist; `uncertain` counts show in the health block. The email classification reads the request host as well as the error class, because `send_mail` writes `email_log` through the same httpx stack after Graph accepts (8.5 step 6). The Teams classification matches substrings of the helper's message text (`teams_alerts.py:350-355`, `:381`, `:450`, `:469`, `:490`, `:494`, `:496`); a test pins those strings so a wording change fails the build. Claims are one row at a time with a 600 s lease, and a lost settle fence is logged, never re-sent.
4a. Teams reachability: a missing `teams_alert_users` row means on, and the first send may fail `permanent` ("No Microsoft 365 account found", `teams_alerts.py:381`) or wait hours for a policy install; that person gets no router Teams message until resolved, keeps the bell and Inbox item, gets no email in its place, and sees "Teams not reachable yet" on the Alerts card (owner question 35). The two `@g3test.dev` e2e accounts probably have no Microsoft 365 account, so the live Teams leg may only prove the failure path (11.5 step 9b).
4b. The RFP test session hold (8.5 step 2) pauses all router sends while a test session is active; rows wait `pending` and go after the session ends, so a long session delays them. Accepted: it is the same fail-closed rule BDR's own Teams path uses. The send-time gates (8.5 step 4) bound the delay: a Teams row more than 3 hours old or a digest email past its catch-up window settles `stale`, and a non-urgent Teams row released inside quiet hours waits for 06:00.
4c. Email volume: a person with many normal items gets one digest email per slot with every item (no cap, owner question 16), and at most two per workday.
5. Users with no delegation get inbox rows (D7) and may be surprised by a badge. Mitigation: the badge appears only when `features.assistant_inbox` is on; pilot via the allowlist.
6. Two workers and clock skew: Python passes `p_now`; workers on Railway share NTP time. Fences make skew harmless for duplicates; at worst a slot opens a few seconds early on one worker.
