# Bid Intelligence record (research draft)

Status: RESEARCH DRAFT, 2026-09-23. Nothing is built. No migration, no code, no flag. This doc and `BID_INTELLIGENCE_SCHEMA.json` capture what an LLM should pull from every bid package and how to store it, for a future build.

The full report (every field with why it matters, where it lives, sources, verified Nevada facts) is the published "Bid Intelligence Catalog" page. The schema file carries the same fields: each property has `title`, `description` (what, why, where), `x-tier`, `x-priority`, `x-source-kind`, `x-extraction-notes`, and `x-reference-facts` where fact-checked statutes apply.

## How it was produced

10 web researchers (415 candidate fields, about 450 sources), 7 section mergers, a Nevada law fact-checker and a technical standards fact-checker (76 claims checked against primary sources; 34 corrected, 3 wrong), a completeness critic, 7 section reviewers acting as a Las Vegas chief estimator, and a rollout planner. Result: 257 fields in 15 sections.

Tier 1 = check on every project (silence recorded as not_stated); Tier 2 = extract when addressed; Tier 3 = optional.

## Short list

**Do not get disqualified**

- `bid_submission.gc_sub_bid_cutoffs`: The GC's cutoff, not the owner's bid time, is our real deadline; a number that lands after it is never compared on price.
- `bid_submission.submission_channels`: A bid sent to the wrong portal or address, or missing the hard-copy original on a hybrid bid, is treated as late.
- `bid_submission.addenda_acknowledgment`: An unacknowledged addendum is one of the most common avoidable disqualifications, even when it changed nothing for us.
- `bid_submission.pre_bid_meetings`: Missing a mandatory meeting, or a separate mandatory walk with its own sign-in, voids the bid regardless of price.
- `bid_submission.bid_attachments_required`: One missing form, affidavit or certificate rejects more bids than pricing errors do.
- `bid_submission.bid_bond`: No bond where one is required is an instant rejection, and a same-day bond request can fail.
- `bid_submission.exclusions_qualifications_allowed`: On a clean-bid job our usual exclusions can get the bid thrown out, so each one must be priced or settled by RFI before the cutoff.
- `bid_submission.required_license_classifications`: Scope outside our NSCB classifications (fire alarm, low voltage, solar) voids the bid or has to go to a licensed sub.
- `low_voltage_life_safety.certified_installer_programs`: If we are not in the named program we cannot issue the specified system warranty, so it is a go/no-go gate before estimating starts.

**Price the scope right**

- `scope_responsibility.gc_trade_package_by_gc`: Each GC splits the job differently; one number sent to every GC is high for some and missing scope for others.
- `scope_responsibility.responsibility_matrix`: Boundaries with mechanical, site, owner vendors and NV Energy are the largest source of unpriced scope and post-award fights.
- `labor_and_compliance.prevailing_wage_basis`: The wage base is our biggest labor driver; open-shop rates on public work, or a missed federal overlay, is a direct loss.
- `bid_submission.alternates`: Owners award on base bid plus chosen alternates, so a guessed or blank alternate can flip the award.
- `bid_submission.substitution_prior_approval`: Missing the pre-bid approval date locks us to basis-of-design gear, fixture and generator prices and lead times.
- `specified_products.products`: Sole-source vs or-equal is the best single predictor of material cost swing and decides whether we can shop gear and lighting.
- `wiring_and_lighting.conductor_material`: Copper-only vs aluminum-allowed is one of the biggest feeder cost swings, and it often differs between feeders and branch circuits.
- `wiring_and_lighting.wiring_method_permissions`: An EMT-only spec where we assumed MC is a labor swing that turns a winning number into a loss.
- `power_distribution.nv_energy_service_requirements`: A bare 'per NVE' callout means customer-built trench, conduit, pads and vaults under Rule 9 that nobody priced.
- `schedule_and_site.off_hours_work_items`: Most Las Vegas remodels are part night work at premium pay and lower output; one blended rate loses the bid or the money.
- `testing_commissioning_closeout.commissioning_level`: Cx hours are the MEP scope GCs most often find missing at leveling; we either price it or state it.

**Protect the margin**

- `contract_terms.subcontract_form_status`: Without the subcontract every contract term is priced blind, so we request it before bid day or qualify the number.
- `contract_terms.liquidated_damages`: Electrical sits on the critical path; a daily LD rate that reaches us can exceed our whole fee within weeks of a gear delay.
- `contract_terms.payment_contingency`: A true pay-if-paid clause can leave us unpaid for finished work when a private owner fails.
- `contract_terms.material_price_escalation`: With no escalation clause every copper and gear price move after award comes straight out of our margin.
- `contract_terms.sub_bond_requirement`: Bond premium belongs in the number, and a full P&P bond ties up far more surety capacity than the bid bond.
- `contract_terms.wrap_up_program`: Leaving wrapped insurance cost in the price loses the job; assuming coverage that is not wrapped leaves us exposed.
- `power_distribution.long_lead_equipment`: Gear and generator lead times now often run past the GC schedule; we qualify, ask for early release or re-source before LDs bite.

**Know the job**

- `project_profile.owner`: Owner type switches on the Nevada public-works rules (prevailing wage, sub listing, bidder's preference) and sets payment risk.
- `project_profile.work_type`: Renovation carries demolition, discovery, occupied-building and outage costs that new work does not.
- `document_control.issue_status`: A 50 percent or pricing set will change, so contingency and our 'based on documents dated' line have to say so.
- `document_control.addenda_log`: Late addenda are where copper-only flips, feeder upsizes and closed manufacturer lists hide.
- `low_voltage_life_safety.systems_inventory`: Shows at a glance which systems we price in full, as cable and pathway, or as empty conduit for someone else's vendor.

**Learn over time**

- `history_and_outcome.bid_result_market_data`: Spread against the winner by GC, building type and estimator tells us whether we price high or low.
- `history_and_outcome.award_and_debrief`: Separates losing on our price from losing because our GC lost the prime, which a raw win rate hides.
- `history_and_outcome.change_orders_traced_to_documents`: Shows which fields actually cost us money and whether extraction caught them, so the record gets better every job.

## Evidence envelope

Every extracted field in the Bid Intelligence record uses one wrapper, keyed '<section>.<field_key>' (for example bid_submission.substitution_request_deadline). The catalog's value_type describes only `value`; everything else is the wrapper, and every wrapper carries all of its members (the example below shortens the second and third wrappers). Members: value (typed per the catalog; null when status is not_stated, referenced_not_provided, not_applicable, not_found or pending; for conflicting it holds the working value chosen under `resolution`). status (list below). citations: array of {id, file_id (project_files.id), document (file name), doc_role (invitation | rfp | specification | addendum | contract | one of the drawing discipline categories | other), page (1-based physical PDF page, used only to open the viewer), page_label (printed page, e.g. '26 05 19-3'), spec_section ('26 05 19'), article (CSI Part.Article.Paragraph, '2.1.A'), sheet ('E-601'), sheet_revision, location (schedule, table row, keyed note, detail: 'Panel schedule LP-1A', 'Keyed note 4', 'Detail 3/E-501'), addendum (number when the source is an addendum or a sheet or page it reissued), quote (verbatim, about 300 characters max, never paraphrased), quote_verified (true only when code found the quote in the stored page text), bbox (optional, for highlighting)}. Rows of list fields (submittal_register, responsibility_matrix, fixture and panel schedules) carry cite: ['c3', 'c4'] ids that point into this array instead of repeating citation objects. computed_from: field keys used, derived fields only. searched: every location read for this field (sections, Div 00 documents, sheet types, the invitation); required when status is not_stated or referenced_not_provided so silence is provable. confidence: high | medium | low, set by pipeline rules (quote verified, literal vs interpreted, second read agrees), not by the model's self-report alone. note: one line on the interpretation used. verify_action: null or {kind: rfi | ask_gc | request_document | site_walk | check_reference | legal_review, text}. superseded_by_addendum: ordered list, oldest first, empty when the base documents still govern; each entry {addendum, issued, prior_value, prior_citation_ids} records a value an addendum replaced, so the page can show what changed. conflicts: every still-current position that disagrees, [{value, citation_ids, doc_role}]. resolution: null, or {rule: precedence_clause | latest_addendum | most_stringent | reviewer, detail, clause_citation_id} explaining the working value. review: {state: unreviewed | confirmed | corrected | disputed | stale, by, at, llm_value (the model's value, kept when a person corrects it), note}; stale means a later addendum touched the cited pages after the field was confirmed. run: {run_id, package_version (e.g. 'base+ADD-1..3'), model (llm.active_model label, 'self-hosted:...' when local), prompt_version, extracted_at}. Partial objects: when an object is partly stated (LD amount stated, unit silent) the envelope status is found and the silent sub-property uses the catalog's own not_stated enum or null; an envelope-level not_stated means the whole field is silent. Dates and times are ISO 8601 with an explicit offset (America/Los_Angeles only when the text says local time for a Nevada site; otherwise flag), money is a number in USD, percent is 0-100, durations keep their unit and whether days are calendar or working. 'Needs review' is deliberately NOT a status: it is computed for the review queue from status (conflicting, not_found, referenced_not_provided), low confidence, an unverified quote, critical and unreviewed, changed by the latest addendum, or stale.

Statuses:

- found: stated in the documents or the GC invitation, with at least one citation whose verbatim quote code has verified against the page text
- inferred: not stated in words but follows from cited text by a named rule (manufacturers listed with no 'or equal' means no substitution; a 20-25 year cabling system warranty implies a manufacturer-certified installer; an NV Energy standard detail callout implies customer-built trench, conduit and pad). Needs a citation plus a note naming the rule. Never allowed for money, percent, date, time, duration or quantity values
- computed: derived by code from other fields (days from invitation to bid, absolute RFI deadline from 'N days before bid', quantity roll-ups, bid_brief items, disqualification checks); carries computed_from and inherits the weakest status and confidence of its inputs
- not_stated: the documents are silent; every routed location listed in searched was read and was readable, and none addresses the field. This is a risk finding (price conservatively, carry an exclusion or qualification, or send an RFI), not a data gap
- referenced_not_provided: the package points to a document that governs this field but that document is not in the package (Owner-Contractor Agreement with the LD rate, prime contract, OCIP wrap manual, BIM execution plan, NV Energy standard, wage determination, geotech or hazmat survey). Action: request the document
- conflicting: two or more current (not superseded) sources disagree, including invitation vs owner documents, drawings vs specs, and Div 23 vs Div 26. value holds the working value per resolution; conflicts lists every position with citations
- not_applicable: the subject does not exist on this project (no generator, ground-up so no fire watch or existing-conditions disclaimer); needs a citation or a computed reason
- not_found: the extractor could not establish an answer: pages unreadable or image-only with no OCR, a routed section missing from the package, a critical field whose quote failed verification, or a failed model call. Always routed to a person
- entered: typed by a team member (team_entry fields such as history_and_outcome, or a fact a reviewer added that no document states); carries review.by and a citation when the person gives one
- pending: not yet extracted for this package version, waiting on a model that is away, or a team_entry field not yet filled

## Principles

- **Silence is a finding; failure is not.** not_stated and not_found must never collapse into one blank. not_stated is allowed only when every routed location was read and was readable, and the searched list proves it. not_found means the machine could not tell and a person must look. Tier 1 fields always end a finished run with a real status (never pending or empty), and the bid brief treats a not_stated critical item as a risk to price, exclude, qualify or RFI, while not_found is work for a reviewer.
- **Cite or it did not happen.** Every found, inferred, conflicting and not_applicable value carries at least one citation with a verbatim quote. Code checks the quote against the stored page text (whitespace, hyphenation and ligature tolerant) and checks that every number, date or amount in the value appears in the quote. A critical field without a verified quote is downgraded to not_found and queued; the project page never shows an uncited critical number as fact.
- **Never guess a number.** Money, percent, dates, times, durations, quantities, limits and thresholds come only from literal text in this project's package. No defaults from AIA forms, the named subcontract form, statutes, industry norms, prior projects or model memory. Relative text ('10 days before bid', '5% of bid') is stored as written and the absolute value is a computed field with its inputs. Statutory reference facts (NRS 338 listing thresholds, Nevada retainage caps, apprentice ratios) live in a maintained reference table, are attached by rule and labeled as reference, and are never written into a document field.
- **Keep the literal trigger words.** The words that change price or eligibility are captured in the quote and drive the enum: mandatory vs strongly encouraged, provide vs furnish vs install, or equal vs approved equal vs no substitutions, shall vs should, calendar vs working days, spare vs space, allowance vs unit price. The enum sits on top of the quote so a reviewer sees why it was chosen; 'install' used loosely and Division 01 'Contractor' (usually the GC) are flagged rather than silently resolved.
- **Addenda supersede item by item, in issue order.** Apply addenda oldest to newest to exactly the section, article or sheet each one touches; everything else in the base set still governs. A page or sheet reissued 'in its entirety' replaces its predecessor but is diffed, because revision clouds are dropped once superseded. The replaced value is kept in superseded_by_addendum. RFI-answer-only addenda change no sheet but can assign scope ('the electrical subbidder is responsible for...') and still must be acknowledged. Supersession by an addendum is not a conflict.
- **Order of precedence is read, never assumed.** Extract the project's precedence clause first (Agreement, General or Supplementary Conditions, Instructions to Bidders). AIA A201 ranks nothing (documents are complementary; whatever any one requires is required), while ConsensusDocs 200 and FAR 52.236-21 (specs govern) do rank. Even when a clause names a winner, both positions stay visible. With no clause, the working value is the more stringent or costlier reading until an RFI or addendum answers it.
- **Anchor to document structure, not page numbers.** Spec facts cite section plus Part.Article.Paragraph (26 05 19 2.1.A); drawing facts cite sheet number, revision and the schedule, note or detail; keyed notes are keyed by (sheet, note number) because numbers repeat per sheet. Page numbers shift when addenda reissue pages while CSI structure stays stable, so the physical page is stored only to open the viewer.
- **Extract per section through a field routing map.** Each catalog field has a routing entry: the CSI sections, Division 00 documents, sheet types and invitation parts where it lives, plus keywords. The extractor reads each routed section whole, once, and answers only the fields routed to it. Facts that are scattered by design (manufacturers and or-equal terms, warranties, attic stock, submittals, mockups, certified-installer and delegated-design language) are collected from every Div 26/27/28 section's Part 1 and Part 2 articles, not only from Division 01. Never one prompt over the whole book.
- **Read outside Division 26 on purpose.** Many electrical answers live elsewhere: Div 00 and 01, 07 (firestopping, roofing), 09 (painting of conduit and gear), 21 and 23 (VFDs, starters, controls, equipment schedules), 31 to 33 (trench, backfill, paving), architectural reflected ceiling plans (ceiling types), civil utility sheets (trench runs, NV Energy details), the code or cover sheet (ERRCS, code edition), the GC scope exhibit and the invitation email. The routing map includes them and searched shows they were read.
- **The invitation is a first-class document.** The GC's email or portal message carries the GC cutoff time, trade package, scope exhibit, bond flow-down and attachment rules that are not in the plans and specs. It is stored with the project, cited by sender and date like a page, and when it differs from the owner documents both positions are kept (conflicting), because the GC's rule governs G3's submission.
- **One owner per fact.** Follow the section map: who furnishes, installs or pays lives only in scope_responsibility; manufacturers, basis of design and substitution terms only in specified_products; the general warranty in contract_terms, system warranties and attic stock in testing_commissioning_closeout. Other sections reference the owner field by key. Summary rows that restate another field (for example the matrix's starters_vfds row vs starter_vfd_split_by_tag) are checked by a validator for agreement.
- **Facts before judgment.** Extraction records what the documents say. Legal conclusions (enforceability of pay-if-paid, broad-form indemnity, statutory overrides, venue) get verify_action legal_review, never an assertion. The bid_brief is generated only from record fields; every brief line names the field keys it came from and may not introduce a fact that is not in the record.
- **Trust drawing text, not drawing symbols.** Schedules, general and keyed notes, title blocks and one-line labels are read reliably by vision models; symbol counting is not (AECV-Bench: about 95 percent on text reading vs 34 to 55 percent on symbol counts). AI counts are first-pass estimates labeled as such and never priced without the estimator's takeoff; schedule quantity vs plan count disagreements are flagged, not averaged.
- **Store values that differ by area as separate rows.** When a value differs by building, phase, area, feeder vs branch circuit, power vs control wiring, ICRA zone or equipment tag, store separate rows with applies_to instead of one blended answer. Blending is how 'aluminum allowed' leaks from feeders into branch circuits.
- **Reviewer decisions are sticky and every run is versioned.** A confirmed or corrected value is never overwritten by a re-run. A re-run that disagrees records a proposal and, if its cited pages changed, marks the field stale for re-review. Every run records package version, model label and prompt version, and the record keeps its history across addenda so the team can see what was priced and what changed after.
- **Code does the deterministic work; the model does meaning.** Section headers, Part and Article numbering, sheet numbers, title blocks with a text layer, date and money normalization, quote verification, supersession, precedence application, roll-ups and disqualification rules are code. Model output is validated against a strict schema with closed enums that include not_stated; invalid output is retried, never coerced into a valid-looking value.

## Pipeline

1. **Snapshot the package version.** Triggered when a project's files are promoted (RFP pipeline) or uploaded (New Bid, addendum, revision). Freeze the current set of project_files ids, byte hashes and categories, excluding is_source_set rows, plus the invitation text. Every later step writes against this package_version (base, base+ADD-1, ...), so each record answers 'as of which documents'.
2. **Triage (reuse the Bid File Splitter).** Use the splitter's output as the document map: drawing sets cut by discipline, specifications, addenda and RFP documents left intact, non-PDFs classified. Nothing new to build here; the intel run only reads categories. Addendum segments with a null number or date get a proposed number and date from step 5 for the Estimating Admin to accept.
3. **Text layer, OCR and page store.** For every PDF page extract text with positions (a PyMuPDF-style reader) and store per page: text, hash, has_text_layer, rotation. Image-only pages go to OCR or a vision transcription and are flagged lower confidence. This store is what quotes are verified against and what the viewer highlights, and it is reused across runs by hash.
4. **Index the spec book and the sheet set (deterministic first).** Specs: regex for 'SECTION 26 05 19', 'END OF SECTION', PART 1/2/3, articles (1.1, 2.3) and paragraphs, page labels; also legacy 5-digit 16xxx numbering and Division 00 documents that do not follow CSI. Output: section number, title, page range, parts present, article list; mismatches with the table of contents go to document_control. Drawings: sheet number, title, discipline, revision and date from each title block, compared with the cover-sheet index; tag pages that hold one-lines, panel, fixture, feeder, equipment connection and mechanical schedules, general notes, keyed notes and the code sheet. The model only repairs ambiguous boundaries.
5. **Addenda pass: build the supersession map.** For each addendum in issue order extract number, date, and an item list {target section/article or sheet, change type (revised, added, deleted, reissued in entirety, RFI answer, clarification), summary}. For reissued pages and sheets, diff new text and schedules against the prior version because clouds disappear. Output the supersession map (which page or sheet is current for each section and sheet) and the addenda log with bid-form, bid-date and scope-reassignment flags ('responsible for' or 'shall include' next to a trade name).
6. **Resolve the routing map for this project.** A static config lists, per catalog field, the CSI sections, Div 00 documents, sheet types, invitation parts and keywords where it may appear. Resolve it against the index and supersession map to the actual current pages, and add keyword-gated sections from Divisions 07, 09, 21, 23 and 31 to 33 (a cheap small-model or keyword check decides relevance). The resolved list becomes each field's searched list.
7. **Front-end documents pass.** Read the invitation, Instructions to Bidders, bid form, Agreement, General and Supplementary Conditions and any GC subcontract or exhibits whole (one document per call) for bid_submission, labor_and_compliance, contract_terms, project_profile and document_control.order_of_precedence. These are short and high stakes, so they go to the strongest document model with a verifier on every critical number.
8. **Per-section spec pass.** One call per current spec section (Div 01, 26, 27, 28 and gated others) with the section text, its article map and only the fields routed to it. Every section also answers a fixed 'scatter checklist': manufacturers and or-equal wording, warranty, extra materials and attic stock, submittals, quality assurance and certified installers, testing, mockups, delegated design, work by others and related sections. Output is candidate values with article-level citations; long lists (submittal rows) come back per section, never as one giant answer.
9. **Drawings pass.** Use the text layer where the CAD export has one; render at high resolution and tile large sheets with overlap where it does not. Extract schedules as rows (panel, fixture, feeder, one-line values, equipment connection, mechanical equipment electrical data), general notes, keyed notes keyed by (sheet, number), code-sheet items, NV Energy detail callouts, civil trench runs, and ceiling types from the architectural RCP. Symbol counts, if run at all, are tier 3 first-pass estimates.
10. **Merge, normalize and validate.** Group candidates per field. Identical values merge with multiple citations; values from a superseding addendum win and push the prior value into superseded_by_addendum; remaining disagreements become conflicting with resolution per the precedence clause or most-stringent rule. Normalize dates (explicit offset), money, percents and units; validate against the catalog schema. Fields with no candidate after every routed page was read become not_stated with searched filled; if a routed page was unreadable or a referenced document is missing, not_found or referenced_not_provided instead.
11. **Citation verification.** For every citation, find the quote in the stored page text and check that the value's numbers and dates appear in it; for critical fields also run an independent verifier call that sees only the quote and the value and answers supports, contradicts or insufficient. Failures lower confidence; a critical field with no supporting quote becomes not_found and is queued.
12. **Cross-document reconciliation pass.** Run last, over extracted fields rather than raw pages, as a fixed list of pairwise checks: fixture schedule vs 26 51 00; panel and one-line AIC vs spec minimums; mechanical schedule vs electrical connection schedule tag by tag; Div 23 vs Div 26 on VFDs, starters and controls wiring; conductor material in specs vs drawing notes; code sheet ERRCS vs any Div 27/28 owner; bid-form alternate text vs drawings; sheet index vs title blocks; table of contents vs actual sections; invitation vs owner documents on dates and scope. Output feeds document_control.plans_vs_specs_conflicts, design completeness flags and conflicting statuses.
13. **Rules, reference data and roll-ups.** Code attaches standing reference facts by rule (NRS 338 checks when the owner is a Nevada public body, statutory retainage limits, license classes), computes derived fields (days from invitation to bid, absolute RFI and substitution deadlines), and builds quantities_rollup from the owning sections' schedules with its computation basis.
14. **Bid brief.** Rules build the disqualification checklist (mandatory meetings, bond, addenda acknowledgment, attachments, licenses, EMR limit), exclusions to carry (scope rows not_stated or conflicting), RFIs to send (conflicting and critical not_stated), documents to request (referenced_not_provided) and go/no-go signals mapped to BDR's rubric. A model only words the top-risk sentences, each tied to field keys.
15. **Human review.** A queue ordered critical first, then by status (conflicting, not_found, referenced_not_provided), low confidence, unverified quote and 'changed by the latest addendum'. The reviewer sees the value, the quote and the page with the quote highlighted, and can confirm, correct (typed value plus citation), dispute with a note, or mark not applicable. Tier 1 critical fields must be confirmed or corrected before the record is marked reviewed.
16. **Re-run on new addenda and corrections.** A new addendum, revision or splitter correction creates a new package version. The addenda pass finds the touched sections and sheets; only fields routed to them are re-extracted (unchanged sections reuse cached results by text hash), then reconciliation, roll-up and brief re-run. The run produces a 'what changed in Addendum N' diff; confirmed fields whose citations were touched go stale; a change to a tier 1 critical field (bid date or time, bid form, alternates, substitution deadline, bond, mandatory meeting) raises a notification.
17. **After the bid.** The team enters history_and_outcome (result, rank, spread, debrief, change orders traced back to bid documents). Reviewer corrections and traced change orders are stored as training and evaluation examples for the next prompt or model version.

## Model guidance

Principle: use the cheapest thing that is reliable for each step, measure it on the golden set, and never let a model do what code can do.

1) No model (most of the pipeline). Page text with positions, CSI section and Part/Article parsing, sheet-number and title-block parsing where a text layer exists, the supersession map, date/money/unit normalization, quote verification, precedence application, rules, reference data, roll-ups and the disqualification checklist are code. This is also where most of the trust comes from.

2) Small self-hosted model (today qwen-3.5-4b, FP8 on the L4 box, 65,536-token server context, thinking disabled server-side, vision-capable since the Bid File Splitter runs on it). Good for: page and document classification (already the splitter), relevance gates ('does section 23 05 13 say anything about VFDs, disconnects or controls wiring?'), locating candidate paragraphs, reading title-block fields, and literal single-value fields inside one short section with a tight schema. Keep every call to one section or one sheet, well below the server limit. Do not use it for inferred statuses, legal clauses, addenda diffs, conflicts, dense multi-column schedules, or any critical field without a stronger verifier. Its quality on BDR features is unverified at 4B, so no field routes to it until the golden set says so. Keep thinking off for it (with thinking on it spent the whole max_tokens budget on reasoning).

3) Frontier hosted document model (the class BDR already uses for the document-heavy boq feature). Use it for: the front-end documents (invitation, ITB, bid form, Agreement, General and Supplementary Conditions, subcontract), per-section extraction of critical sections (Div 00, 01 general requirements, 26 05 xx, gear, generators, 27 and 28), addenda diffs, inferred statuses, the reconciliation pass, bid-brief wording, and the verifier. Long context lets it take a whole Division 00 or a 60-page section in one call, but still send one section per call plus only the routed field schemas. Attention to the middle of very long inputs is weaker, per-section calls give article-level citations, and re-runs on addenda then re-read only the touched sections. Allow extended reasoning only on the addenda and conflict passes.

4) Vision. Drawings first use the text layer, which vector CAD exports usually have. Vision is for layout (reading schedules as tables) and scanned sheets. Render at high resolution and tile large sheets with overlap; one downscaled image of a 30x42 sheet makes ratings illegible. Use a frontier vision model for panel, fixture, feeder, one-line and equipment connection schedules, and the small model only for sheet-type tagging. Symbol counting is optional, tier 3, and labeled as an estimate.

5) Second reads for critical numbers. For tier 1 critical numeric and date fields (bid due and GC cutoff, bond percent, LD rate, retainage, DBE goal, substitution deadline, insurance limits), run an independent check: a verifier call that sees only the quote and the value, or a second extraction with a different model or prompt. Disagreement goes to review. This is cheap because it sends a sentence, not a section.

Cost awareness (no vendor prices assumed here). The spec book is the bulk of the tokens: a dense text page is on the order of several hundred tokens, so a 600 to 1,000 page book is hundreds of thousands of input tokens. Feeding the whole book to a frontier model on every addendum is the expensive mistake. Controls: route only relevant sections (Div 00, 01, 26, 27, 28 plus keyword-gated sections from 07, 09, 21, 23 and 31 to 33); cache results by section text hash so an unchanged section is never re-extracted across addenda or re-runs; keep the fixed system prompt and field schemas as a stable prefix (vLLM prefix caching is already on; hosted providers offer prompt caching); use batch or offline processing for first runs that are not urgent where the provider supports it; escalate from the small model to the frontier model only on relevance or low confidence. Record tokens and duration per project in llm_call_log so real cost per bid is measured, not guessed.

BDR routing specifics. Add feature keys to services/llm.py per step, each env-selectable like bid_split (anthropic | openai | self_hosted) so models can be compared step by step: for example bid_intel_route (small), bid_intel_extract (sections and front-end documents), bid_intel_drawings (vision), bid_intel_reconcile (addenda and conflicts), bid_intel_verify, bid_intel_brief. Respect the current limits: the self-hosted complete_pdf_json path extracts text with pypdf, capped at 50 pages and 200,000 characters, and raises on image-only scans, and complete_images_json has no text fallback. So the pipeline sends its own per-section text and tiles, never whole PDFs. With FULL_SELF_HOSTED_LLMS_ENABLED on, nothing may go to a hosted model. Frontier-tier steps then either run on the self-hosted model with confidence capped at medium and review forced, or wait as pending (the RFP pipeline's wait-never-fall-back rule), chosen per step by setting and never silently.

## Where it plugs into BDR

- **Where it runs in the RFP pipeline.** Today the pipeline goes harvest, then split (Bid File Splitter), then create, then rfp_create_files promotion. Bid Intelligence starts after promotion completes, as a background job, so project creation is never delayed. Manual New Bid projects start it when their first files land. The project is born at go_no_go, and the record's first pass is ready to inform that decision.
- **Runs on the existing LLM queue.** Each step is an llm_jobs job (new job types such as bid_intel_index, bid_intel_section, bid_intel_drawings, bid_intel_reconcile, bid_intel_brief) with the queue's leases, retry ladder, outage wait and one-active-job-per-target rule. Every call lands in llm_call_log and shows on the dev AI Monitor, which gives cost and failure visibility for free.
- **Reuses the splitter and file categories.** Categories come from project_files (drawings by discipline, specification, addendum, rfp, other), with is_source_set rows excluded. A splitter correction that re-files documents (rfp_split.resync_project_files) or a new addendum or revision upload creates a new package version and triggers an incremental re-run. Addendum segments the splitter left without a number or date get a proposed number and date from the addenda pass, which the Estimating Admin accepts.
- **The invitation is already stored.** rfp_emails and rfp_portal_invitations hold the GC's message, and the rfp_extract step already pulls project name and dates from it. The intel run cites that stored message as the invitation document and merges with rfp_extract's values, not a second parallel extraction.
- **New storage (dev database first).** Proposed tables, created by a migration applied to the dev BDR database only (never BDR_Prod without explicit approval): bid_intel_runs (project, package_version, file set hash, model and prompt version per step, status, timings); bid_intel_fields (project, field_key, section, envelope JSONB, current flag, run, review state, reviewer), which is queryable for cross-project analytics; bid_intel_pages (file, page, text, hash, OCR flag) for quote verification and highlighting; bid_intel_spec_sections and bid_intel_sheets (the index); bid_intel_corrections (training examples). The full record per version can also be kept as one JSONB snapshot for export.
- **Project page: Bid Intelligence panel.** A tab on the project showing the bid brief at the top (disqualification checklist, top risks, exclusions to carry, RFIs to send, documents to request), then tier 1 fields by section with status chips (Found, Inferred, Not stated, Not provided, Conflict, N/A, Could not read). Every citation opens the existing file preview at the cited page with the quote highlighted (keep the PDF iframe un-sandboxed, as today). Everything follows BDR's existing UI kit and Pacific-time display.
- **Review queue.** A 'Needs review (n)' list per project, critical first, with Confirm, Correct (value plus citation), Dispute and Not applicable actions, plus a cross-project queue for the Estimating Admin like the dashboard task list. Writer roles review; the accountant is view-only; external estimators do not see the record (it holds contract terms and internal risk notes) unless a technical subset is later chosen for the estimator portal.
- **Existing BDR fields: suggest, never overwrite.** Where the record overlaps stored columns (actual bid due, invitation date, labor_time, wage_type, bid_notes, est start and finish, is_rebid, bidding_url, GCs bidding, the go/no-go rubric answers), show 'Documents say X, project has Y' with a one-click accept. The Go/No-Go review gets suggested rubric answers with citations (owner_type, bid_method, labor_needed, subs_needed, est_value_band, scope_fit), never auto-filled.
- **Addenda: 'what changed' and notifications.** When a new addendum version runs, the project gets a 'What changed in Addendum N' card next to the Plans & Specs Log, and a bell notification when a tier 1 critical field changes (bid date or time, bid form, alternates, substitution deadline, bond, mandatory meeting). Confirmed fields whose citations changed show as stale in the review queue.
- **Downstream use in existing screens.** Suggested, not automatic: exclusions and clarifications from the brief as proposal qualification lines; approved manufacturers and the substitution deadline from specified_products added to the matching vendor RFQ categories (Switchgear, Lighting, Generator & Equipment, Low Voltage) and to the Receive Quotes view; outstanding disqualification items (mandatory pre-bid, bond, addenda acknowledgment, attachments) added to the weekday due digest; history_and_outcome joined to the existing won/lost outcome and per-GC proposal amounts for analytics.
- **Training, testing and monitoring hooks.** A Bid Intelligence tab on the dev-only /training page, like BOQ and the splitter, lists corrections and exports JSONL. The /rfp-testing monitor gains an intel step with per-step events, and the golden-set harness runs as a backend script against dev.
- **Flags and rollout.** A master flag BID_INTEL_ENABLED (default false), plus per-step model settings in llm.py. Start with tier 1 fields and the submittal register (the most proven category) on dev, run the golden set, then widen. It honors FULL_SELF_HOSTED_LLMS_ENABLED strictly (wait or run locally with forced review, never a silent hosted fallback). Staging and prod get the migration and flags only at a release the user approves.

Every one of the 257 is assigned to exactly one phase; check the builder's schema file against this list before coding.

## Evaluation

- **Golden set of past G3 bids.** Pick 20 to 30 projects BDR already holds files for, won and lost, covering: public prevailing-wage, private, resort/casino occupied renovation, healthcare, ground-up vs TI, projects with 2 or more addenda, one design-build fire alarm, one with an NV Energy line extension, and at least a few scanned or poor-quality sets. Keep the addenda sequence so each package version can be tested.
- **Labeling protocol.** An estimator labels every tier 1 field and every critical field per package version with value, status and citation, using the same envelope; a second person checks critical fields. Record the labeling time too: it is the baseline the tool must beat, and label disagreements show which fields are ambiguous even for people.
- **Field-level value metrics.** Per field and per section: exact-match accuracy for enums, numbers, dates and money (after normalization); normalized match for short text; set precision and recall for lists (alternates, allowances, approved manufacturers, attachments, submittal rows, schedule rows). Report per field, never only one blended score, because one bad critical field matters more than ten good low ones.
- **Status confusion matrix.** Compare predicted vs true status. The two errors that cost money: 'not_stated' when the truth is found (a missed fact) and 'found' when the truth is not_stated (a hallucinated fact). Track both rates separately for critical fields, plus correct use of referenced_not_provided and not_applicable.
- **Citation quality.** Automated: share of citations whose quote is found on the cited page and whose section exists in the index. Human-graded sample: does the quote actually support the value? Hallucination rate = values with no supporting text anywhere in the package.
- **Addenda and re-run tests.** Run each golden project version by version and score: fields changed by an addendum correctly updated, prior value kept in superseded_by_addendum, untouched fields unchanged, confirmed fields correctly marked stale only when their pages changed, and the 'what changed' diff complete.
- **Seeded mutation suite.** Cheap regression tests built by editing real packages: add an addendum flipping copper to aluminum; delete 'or equal' from 26 24 16; move the substitution deadline; make the pre-bid meeting mandatory; put an earlier GC cutoff in the invitation; delete a spec section; replace a sheet in its entirety with a changed panel AIC; add 'the electrical subcontractor is responsible for trenching' to an RFI answer. Each mutation must be detected. Run on every prompt or model change.
- **Conflict detection recall.** Label real conflicts in the golden set and add seeded ones (schedule vs spec, Div 23 vs Div 26, invitation vs owner dates). Measure recall first (missed conflicts are the costly error) and precision second (noise erodes trust in the queue).
- **Confidence calibration.** Check accuracy by confidence bucket: high should almost never be wrong. If it is, tighten the rules that assign high instead of trusting the model's self-report.
- **Per-step model bake-off.** Run the same golden set through each step with the small self-hosted model and the hosted models, and record accuracy, tokens and minutes per project. Route each step to the cheapest model that meets its bar; re-run when a model or prompt changes.
- **Proposed trust gates.** Proposal, to be set with the team: every critical field stays review-required until it shows at least 98 percent precision on found values and at least 95 percent recall of truly stated facts on the golden set, with zero hallucinated critical numbers. Tier 2 and 3 fields can display unreviewed with a badge once they reach an agreed lower bar. No prompt or model change ships if a critical field regresses.
- **Production measurement from reviewer actions.** Every review action is a label: confirmed counts as correct, corrected as wrong (keep llm_value), disputed as unclear. Track correction rate per field, per model label and per prompt version over time, and time-to-review per project. Fields with rising correction rates go back to prompt or routing work. A 10 percent blind re-review of confirmed fields by a second estimator measures reviewer error as well.
- **Corrections as training data.** Store each correction with the exact model input (section text), prompt version, model output, corrected value and citation, the same way BOQ corrections feed boq_training_examples. Use them for few-shot retrieval (similar sections from past projects), for new seeded tests, and later for a JSONL export to fine-tune the self-hosted model. Only corrections that carry a citation count as gold.
- **Close the loop with outcomes.** Post-award change orders and scope gaps traced back to bid documents (history_and_outcome) are the real-world missed-fact rate: each one that traces to a field the record had as found or not_stated is a test case, and each that traces to no field is a candidate new catalog field.
