Story (one ordinary workday after Phase 8, with the company triage switch on, so triage runs for everyone who has the assistant; here a Materials engineer): at 9:12 AM BDR rings his bell "Quote received" for bid 26.10.7301 and, a minute later, "Drawing changed" on the same bid. The Phase 7 router records both quietly in his Inbox as record rows, as it does today. Five minutes later the triage loop takes both rows in one batch, builds a fact pack for each (kind, project number, internal due time, hours left, whether he has read the bell, whether BDR already sent him Teams or email, whether his part is still missing, whether the router already has a "Bid due" line for that project), and asks a small priced model (`gpt-6-luna`) one question per row with enum-only answers. The answer for the quote is "BDR already covers this" and nothing is sent; his Inbox row shows a small "Checked: BDR already told you" note. The answer for the drawing change is "suggest: review the changed sheets against your open RFQs", and his Inbox row shows that suggestion with a "Work on this" button. At 2:40 PM, with the bid due at 5:00 PM and the Gear quote still missing, a "Bid due" bell he has not opened gets the answer "escalate, high, reason: due soon and your part is still missing". Code checks only that the answer is well formed (known key, known enums); the model's choice is final (owner question 6 (c)). The run's fenced finish stores the decision and emits one fact to the router in the same transaction; the router applies only the person's own settings (his mute and "not useful" marks), the one-escalation-per-row dedupe, quiet hours and the company switch, rings one assistant bell and sends him one Teams message whose text is a server template filled from router-computed facts ("Bid 26.10.7301 is due today at 5:00 PM and your part is still missing", the last clause included only because the router itself computes that his part is missing), not a copy of BDR's bell and not model text. Had the model chosen urgent, the same message would have gone out even at 9 PM. He clicks "Work on this" on the drawing row: the assistant panel opens with a prefilled sentence built from the suggestion; he presses Send himself and the ordinary chat turn does the work, with every write as an approval card he clicks. That night he turns off the assistant digest email on his Alerts card; the next morning's digest still lands in his Inbox and bell, with no email. Triage of his rows cost about a tenth of a cent that day, drawn from the company's 50 USD pool under the company spend identity, never from his own 15 USD budget.

Phase 8 in one paragraph: a new, separately named triage loop (`app/services/assistant_triage.py`, its own polling loop, outside the three model-free router modules) reads the Phase 7 bell-sourced record rows of everyone who has the assistant while the company triage switch is on (no personal opt-in or opt-out, no delegation, owner questions 14 (b) and 15 (c)), groups each person's new rows into a batch after a short debounce, builds a fact pack per row that holds ids, enums, counts and Pacific times only (no bell message, no names, no free text), and makes one tool-less, schema-bound `gpt-6-luna` call per batch through one small pinned module (`app/services/assistant_triage_model.py`), reserved under a new spend kind `triage` held by a company spend identity, so it counts against the company cap `assistant_settings.global_daily_cap_usd` and against no person's budget (questions 11 (b) and 12 (b); no floor). The model answers per row with enums only: none (with a reason, usually "BDR already covers this"), suggest (an action from a fixed per-kind catalog), or escalate (normal, high or urgent, with a reason enum). Code checks only that the answer is well formed; no fact predicate and no router rule second-guesses the choice (question 6 (c)). It stores the decision on a new `assistant_triage_items` row, shows it on the Inbox row, and, inside the same fenced finish transaction, turns an escalation into one router event of a new router-owned kind `assistant.triage_escalation` (source `emit`), which the existing router delivers with the person's own mute and "not useful" marks, quiet hours (urgent breaks them, the Phase 7 rule) and its send ledger. When the company cap refuses a batch, its rows wait for Pacific midnight and are retried if still under 24 hours old (question 13 (b)). Triage writes no BDR record, sends nothing itself, proposes no card and runs no tool; "Work on this" moves the work into an ordinary chat turn the person starts. The same phase adds the chat assistant's `inbox_list` read tool (tainted results), a per-person digest email opt-out on the Alerts card, and the `system` actor stamp on the router's digest emails in `email_log`. New strings ship in English only. Stage 2 (an unattended prep turn) is not built (question 1 (a)).

### 3.2 Why a separate loop and two modules

- The router tick must stay short and model-free (its pins forbid model imports, `tests/test_assistant_phase7_pins.py:33-56`). A model call takes seconds and can fail slowly; running it in the router tick would delay digests and sends. A separate loop also keeps "the router calls no model" literally true.
- `app/services/assistant_triage.py` holds the loop, claims, fact gathering, the breaker and decision apply (the emit itself happens inside the finish RPC). It is on the no-model scan list. `app/services/assistant_triage_model.py` is about 80 lines: reserve, attributed call, settle, return. A new pin asserts it is the only module outside `app/assistant/loop.py`, `llm_messages.py`, `assistant_mail_review.py` and the existing feature callers that names the `assistant_triage` feature, and that it never names a sender, `loop`, `jobs`, `executor` or `prompts`.
- Inline model call, not an `llm_jobs` job: a new job type needs a CHECK append (`scripts/check_llm_jobs_constraint_relists.py`), a `_FEATURE_BY_TYPE` entry and a claim pass in `app/services/llm_queue.py:117-126,143,938-955`, and shares the assistant concurrency of 2 with chat turns. ### 3.3 How escalation squares "the app owns notifications" with "whether or not the app already notified them"

(Owner question 6 (c), 2026-11-02: "No rules. The model's choice is final.") The owner wants the assistant free to escalate even when BDR already told the person, and chose to let the model alone decide whether an escalation adds something. What remains of the reconciliation is what an escalation may say, not whether it may land:

1. An escalation never repeats BDR's notification. Its title and why are assistant templates keyed by a reason enum ("is due today at 5:00 PM", "has waited unread since 9:12 AM Monday"), with every slot (absolute Pacific time, part wording, project number) filled from facts the router computes itself at route time, never from the model. It never carries the bell's message, type label sentence or subject. A factual clause ("and your part is still missing", "unread") is included only when the router's own live facts say it is true; otherwise the neutral variant ("Your assistant flagged an update on bid {number}") is used, so a model's reason is never stated to a person as fact (Guiding-principle call, 19.1 FQ5). This is wording, not a rule: the escalation is delivered either way.
2. The model sees whether BDR already reached the person (bell read, bell dismissed, Teams sent at `notifications.teams_sent_at` from 0175, email sent through `notifications.email_log_id`, and whether the router's own `bid.due_not_ready` row exists for the project) and is told in the prompt to escalate only when its reason adds something the app did not say. Nothing checks that it did (no fact predicates).
3. The router applies only the person's own controls and the company's switches: the person's mute of the kind or project and the "not useful" auto-downgrade (R25 and `feedback_auto`, kept by the owner), the person's Teams switch (R23), quiet hours (urgent breaks them), the company triage switch and, by recommendation, the existing closed-project rule R24 (19.1 FQ1). The draft's R27, R28, R31, R32 and the read-bell cap are removed; an escalation and the router's own "Bid due" line can both reach the person (question 28 (c)).
4. At most one escalation per record row (dedupe), and only bell-sourced record rows are triaged, so triage can never invent a stream of pushes from nothing; the company fault fuse (P10) stops a broken prompt or model. (`project.failed_to_bid` is a record kind with no bell, from the audit tail, `proactive.py:342-346`; it is not enqueued, P2.)

A fixed id therefore works through every existing spend and attribution path unchanged, where a null would need a NOT NULL drop, an `is null` branch in two repo reads and a change to the bdr-94-held `llm_messages.py`. The value has version nibble 0, so `gen_random_uuid()` (version 4, the source of every `profiles.id`) can never produce it; the guard above refuses to apply if a profile ever had it. What it gives, with no change to `assistant_spend_state` (`0186_assistant_browser_foundation.sql:306-357`): a triage call logs `user_id` = the company id and `assistant_run_id` = the triage run id, so it counts in `global_spent` (the `assistant_run_id is not null` branch, `:330-333`) and never in a person's `user_spent` (`l.user_id = p_user_id`, `:328-329`); a live triage hold counts in `global_held` and in no person's `user_held` (`:344-352`). Python mirrors it as `spend.COMPANY_SPEND_ID`, pinned equal to the SQL value (16.1). `repo.ensure_profile` and every per-person read refuse the company id (a pinned guard), so it never gets an `assistant_profiles` row, a per-person cap or an Inbox. One existing path does not work unchanged and must exclude the id (MC-1, F3): the daily orphan sweep. `repo.owners_with_open_state(None)` (`repo.py:1971-1987`) lists every `user_id` with a reservation whose `status = 'active'`, and a hold keeps that status after it expires until something settles or releases it (`0174_assistant_phase2.sql:62-64`, "an expired hold needs no sweeper"), so a live triage hold, or one left by a crash between reserve and settle, puts the company id on the list; `run_orphan_sweep` then finds no human profile for it and runs `assistant_offboarding.offboard(company_id, "sweep")` (`assistant_maintenance.py:505`, `:520-526`), which would release live triage holds mid-call through `_release_holds` (`assistant_offboarding.py:251-265`) and run every hook against a non-person. WI-5 makes `owners_with_open_state` drop `spend.COMPANY_SPEND_ID` from the reservations read (`.neq("user_id", COMPANY_SPEND_ID)`), WI-13 makes `assistant_offboarding.offboard` refuse the company id, and the triage purge step releases the company's own expired triage holds that are still `active` (5.1 step 6), so a crash never leaves one open.

The reason and action columns are pattern-checked, not enum-checked, so the catalog in `app/assistant/triage.py` can grow without a CHECK swap; the Python validator is the enum gate (16.1 pins it). It checks format only: the reason picks the escalation template and is the audit label, and nothing checks it against the facts (P6, 19.1 FQ5). `severity_hint` allows `urgent` (P8).

`attempts` counts only answer-quality failures (`bad_answer`, `truncated`, a missing key); infrastructure failures (`model_error`, `timeout`, `busy`, `spend_check`, `unpriced`, `lease_expired`, `breaker`; `unpriced` per 5.4 and 5.7) reschedule with backoff and bump `infra_retries` only, and such an item ends only when it goes stale (5.7). One internal helper owns every return to pending (CCF-10): `assistant_triage_item_retry(p_item_id uuid, p_kind text, p_now timestamptz)` (service_role only like every function here, since the security invoker callers run as service_role; the service never calls it directly, only the claim retirement and the finish do, pinned by a grep test). Three classes: for an answer-quality kind, new attempts = attempts + 1, and at 3 the item becomes `skipped bad_answer` (a missing key or bad answer) or `failed` (`truncated`), otherwise `pending` with `next_attempt_at` from the ladder (60 s, then 5 min); for an infrastructure kind, attempts unchanged, `infra_retries + 1`, `pending` with `next_attempt_at = p_now + least(60 x 2^infra_retries, 900)` seconds; for the kind `cap` (a `cap_global` or `cap_test` refusal, P13), attempts and `infra_retries` unchanged, `cap_deferred_at = coalesce(cap_deferred_at, p_now)`, `pending` with `next_attempt_at` = the next Pacific midnight after `p_now` (`(date_trunc('day', p_now at time zone 'America/Los_Angeles') + interval '1 day') at time zone 'America/Los_Angeles'`) plus a per-item offset of `mod(abs(hashtext(p_item_id::text)::bigint), 7200)` seconds (cast before `abs` so the smallest int cannot overflow), so a large deferred backlog drains between 00:00 and 02:00 PT instead of all at once and the hourly fuse wire stays bounded on a normal night (MC-8; 19.1 FQ10). No path can push `attempts` past 3.

Age-out rule for cap-deferred items (P13, owner question 13 (b)): when such an item is claimed after midnight and its inbox row's `created_at` is 24 hours or more before its deferral midnight, it settles `skipped cap` without a model call (5.3 step 3); one under 24 hours at that midnight is triaged, even when its staggered retry (up to 2 hours past midnight) finds it more than 24 hours old (P8L-2, 2026-11-03). The deferral midnight is the latest Pacific midnight at or before the claim that is after `cap_deferred_at`, and it is the reference only while the claim is inside the stagger plus a 15-minute grace (before 02:15 PT); a claim before any midnight has passed, or after 02:15 PT that day, is judged at the claim time itself (P8L-2-R1, 2026-11-03). That matters because a cap-deferred item keeps `cap_deferred_at` through infrastructure retries (the helper re-pends it with backoff), so without the 02:15 cut a same-day retry after a model error, the breaker or a worker outage was judged at midnight all day and could triage a row up to about 47 hours old; with it no row older than about 26 hours 15 minutes is triaged. The service judges it (`assistant_triage._cap_reference`, constants `CAP_STAGGER` and `CAP_STAGGER_GRACE`), the claim RPC has no age filter, so no migration change was needed. A claim a day late (the worker down past the whole stagger) is judged by the same rule against the later midnight, so the rule never stretches past one night. An item with `cap_deferred_at` set is judged by this 24-hour rule only, never by `assistant_triage_window_hours`, so a window shorter than 24 hours cannot cancel the owner's midnight retry (MC-7). A second cap refusal after midnight defers it again, and by the next midnight it is over 24 hours old, so a row waits at most one night (this holds only because the finish applies per-item skips before the cap handling, A6.3, MC-6).

## 5. Services

### 5.1 Loop and kill switches (`app/services/assistant_triage.py`)

`poll_once(now=None, *, worker=None) -> dict` runs inside `polling_loop` (`asyncio.to_thread`, guarded, every `ASSISTANT_TRIAGE_POLL_SECONDS`, default 60). Each step in its own try (the router's `_step` shape, `assistant_proactive.py:1391-1398`). Steps:

1. Gate: return `{"skipped": "env_off"}` unless `ASSISTANT_ENABLED`, `ASSISTANT_PROACTIVE_ENABLED` and `ASSISTANT_TRIAGE_ENABLED`. Read the company row once: `assistant_settings.enabled`, `proactive_enabled` and `triage_enabled` must all be true, else call `repo.triage_skip_all(None, "switched_off", now, limit=500)` (A6.8; it acts only while the switch is off) and return.
2. Fuse (P10, owner question 10 (a), a company-wide fault guard, not a reach limit): if the same row's latch `triage_fuse_tripped_at` (A2) is set, call `repo.triage_skip_all(None, "fuse", now, limit=500)` and return `{"skipped": "fuse"}` without claiming. The latch is set by the claim RPC itself (A6.2 step 3), checked before every batch on both workers, and stays set until an Executive or IT Admin turns the company triage switch off and on (the switch-on clears it and writes a new `triage_enabled_at`, which empties both wires); nothing clears it by itself (P8R-1, MC-2, F1). The wires: over the last 50 decisions made after `triage_enabled_at`, with at least 20 in that sample, escalations divided by decisions exceed `ASSISTANT_TRIAGE_FUSE_RATIO` (default 0.5); or escalations decided after `triage_enabled_at` within the last hour exceed `ASSISTANT_TRIAGE_FUSE_PER_HOUR` (default 30). Escalations of every severity count, urgent included (19.1 FQ4); retried cap-deferred rows count like any other (19.1 FQ10). On the first trip the service logs once. The health block (8.1) shows it. (No new bell: the app's health surfaces own it. Guiding-principle call.) While the latch is set, escalations already emitted but not yet delivered are withdrawn by the router (5.6; 19.1 FQ9).
3. Breaker (5.7): if the per-process breaker is open, return without claiming; the health block shows "paused: model unreachable" and the next probe time.
4. Enqueue: `repo.triage_enqueue(None, now, window_hours, limit=500, user_ids=allowlist)` (allowlist `None` when the env allowlist is empty, the default).
5. Claim and run up to `ASSISTANT_TRIAGE_BATCHES_PER_TICK` batches (5.2 to 5.5), strictly one claim, model call and finish at a time, passing the same allowlist and the two fuse settings to the claim (`user_ids=allowlist`, `fuse_ratio`, `fuse_per_hour`). A claim answer of `{fuse: true}` or `{switched_off: true}` ends the tick at once (P8R-2: the fuse is evaluated before every claim, not once per tick, so a run of urgent escalations stops after the batch that crosses a wire, plus at most the one batch the other worker has in flight). After the first infrastructure failure in a tick (model error, timeout, `LlmBusy`), stop claiming for that tick and open the breaker. After a cap refusal (`cap_global` or `cap_test`), stop claiming for that tick and pause claims in this process for 15 minutes (a cap pause, so a full pool does not cost one refused reserve per person per tick); each later probe that is refused again defers only that one batch to midnight.
6. Purge once a Pacific day after 02:00 (`repo.triage_purge`); in the same step, release the company's own `triage` holds that are still `status = 'active'` but expired more than an hour ago (a bounded repo read by `spend.COMPANY_SPEND_ID` and kind `triage`, then the existing `spend.settle` path), so a crash between reserve and settle never leaves an open company hold (MC-1).

Kill switch order: `ASSISTANT_ENABLED`, `ASSISTANT_PROACTIVE_ENABLED`, `ASSISTANT_TRIAGE_ENABLED` (loop not started), `assistant_settings.enabled`, `proactive_enabled`, `triage_enabled` (DB, no redeploy; Executive and IT Admin, owner question 11), the optional allowlist, the person's eligibility (active, internal, `assistant_profiles.enabled`, 5.3), the fuse, the breaker, the cap pause. There is no per-person triage switch and no delegation (P14, P15). Escalation delivery then follows the router's own chain (Phase 7 12.1) plus, for this kind only, the company triage switch, its switched-on time and the fuse latch at route, delivery and Teams send time (5.6).

### 5.2 Batch claim

`repo.triage_claim(None, worker, now, lease_seconds, debounce_seconds, batch_max, fuse_ratio, fuse_per_hour, user_ids=allowlist)` (A6.2). Debounce 300 s and batch 10 by default (P5). The lease is 420 s by default and the boot validator requires `assistant_triage_lease_seconds >= llm_background_wait_seconds + assistant_triage_timeout_seconds x (third_party_llm_max_retries + 1) + 60` (180 + 45 x 2 + 60 = 330 with today's defaults, `config.py:511`, `:515-516`), the same rule the RFP lease follows (`config.py:2477-2490`): `complete_json`'s timeout argument only sets `with_options(timeout=...)` (`llm.py:659-660`) and does not turn off the SDK's own retry (`max_retries=settings.third_party_llm_max_retries`, default 1, `llm.py:632`, `:638`), so one 45 s call can take 90 s after a 180 s gate wait. (Decided here: raise the lease rather than add a `max_retries=0` option to `complete_json`, which would touch a shared function's contract.) A run whose real-clock lease expires is retired by the next claim as `failed lease_expired`; its items return to pending without an attempt. A late finish from the old worker is fenced out (`fenced: false`): it writes no decision and emits no escalation, because the emit lives inside the fenced finish (A6.3).

### 5.3 Per-batch checks and the fact pack

For the claimed person, in order (any failure settles the run and every item; no model call happens):

1. Eligibility (P15, owner question 15 (c): no delegation): the router's own test, shared rather than copied: an active profile with an internal role and a working assistant (`access.assistant_enabled_for` over the company row and the person's `assistant_profiles` row, an absent row counting as on), exactly what `_Context.eligible` checks (`assistant_proactive.py:326-346`, "No delegation test (D7)") without the per-kind sub-app step (record rows were already filtered by it when the router wrote them). A problem calls `assistant_triage_skip_user(..., 'ineligible')`, which settles the person's pending and claimed items `skipped ineligible` and fails the run with error kind `ineligible` (A6.4); the service makes no finish call for that run (it would be fenced out anyway). `access.owner_check` is not called (it requires a live delegation, `access.py:149`).
2. The allowlist is not re-checked here as a skip reason: the claim already filtered by it (A6.2), so a claimed person outside this process's allowlist is a bug; the service logs it, releases the run back to pending through a `failed lease_expired`-style finish (an infrastructure kind, no attempt counted) and never settles the items.
3. Per item: re-read the inbox row; dismissed or gone settles that item `skipped dismissed`; a cap-deferred item (`cap_deferred_at` set) whose inbox row was 24 hours old or more at its deferral midnight (A4, P8L-2; at the claim itself when the claim is past 02:15 PT, P8L-2-R1) settles `skipped cap` (P13), and a cap-deferred item under 24 hours old is never judged by the window (MC-7: with `assistant_triage_window_hours` under 24, a 20-hour cap-deferred row would otherwise settle `skipped stale` and cancel the owner's midnight retry); any other item whose row is older than the window, or any item whose `internal_bid_at` has passed for a `bell.due` kind, settles `skipped stale`. These go to the finish as per-item `skip_reason` entries (A6.3); if no item is left, the run finishes without a model call as `decided` with only those skip entries and no usage (A6.3 applies the skips first, so no claimed item is left for the unnamed-item retry).

### 5.4 The model call (`app/services/assistant_triage_model.py`)

```python
def classify(run_id: str, pack: dict, *, settings) -> dict:
    route = llm.resolve("assistant_triage", settings)    # never logged, printed or returned
    if route.provider != "openai" or llm_pricing.price_for(route.model) is None:
        raise TriageUnpriced()                            # fail closed before any reserve: a self-hosted
                                                          # fallthrough would log a NULL cost (llm.py:472-515)
    rendered = triage.render_pack(pack)
    est_in = triage.estimate_input_tokens(triage.SYSTEM_PROMPT, triage.ANSWER_SCHEMA, rendered)  # len/4 of all three
    max_out = min(settings.assistant_triage_max_output_tokens, 1200 + 200 * len(pack["items"]))  # P8L-3
    amount = llm_pricing.estimate_cost(route.model, input_tokens=est_in, output_tokens=max_out)
    if amount is None:
        raise TriageUnpriced()
    test_cap = settings.assistant_triage_test_spend_cap_usd   # None unless set on a dev command line (13)
    if test_cap is not None:
        today = repo.triage_company_spend(None)               # A6.7: {spent, held} under the company id
        if today["spent"] + today["held"] + amount > test_cap:
            raise TriageTestCap()                             # run finishes cap_test; items wait for midnight (P13)
    company = spend.COMPANY_SPEND_ID                          # A2a; never a person (P12)
    ttl = triage_hold_ttl(settings)                           # the lease formula of 5.2, never a fixed 300
    try:
        hold = spend.reserve(company, kind=spend.KIND_TRIAGE, amount=amount, ttl=ttl, run_id=run_id, settings=settings)
    except spend.SpendRefused as exc:
        raise TriageCapRefused() from exc                     # cap_global (always scope global, B2)
    except Exception as exc:
        raise TriageSpendCheck() from exc                     # spend_check, fail closed
    try:
        with llm_gate.tier(llm_gate.TIER_PIPELINE), llm_messages.attributed(company, run_id, reservation_id=hold):
            return llm.complete_json("assistant_triage", system=triage.SYSTEM_PROMPT,
                                     messages=[{"role": "user", "content": rendered}],
                                     schema=triage.ANSWER_SCHEMA, schema_name="triage_answer",
                                     max_tokens=max_out,
                                     timeout=settings.assistant_triage_timeout_seconds, settings=settings)
    except Exception as exc:
        raise _translate(exc) from exc                        # the closed set below (F7)
    finally:
        spend.settle(company, hold)
```

- Exception contract (F7). The triage service may not name `services.llm` (`tests/test_assistant_no_model_unattended.py:42` forbids `\bservices\.llm\b(?!_)`, and 16.1 pins it for the triage service), so it cannot catch `llm.LlmBadOutput` itself, and `LlmBadOutput` (`llm.py:130`) covers both a cut-off answer (`llm.py:902`, `:969`, "Model response was cut off") and invalid JSON (`llm.py:681`). `assistant_triage_model.py` therefore defines a closed set, and `classify` raises only these (the hold is settled in every case):

| Exception | Raised when | Run `error_kind` | Item handling (A4 helper) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `TriageUnpriced` | non-OpenAI route or no price row | `unpriced` | infrastructure |
| `TriageTestCap` | the live-test cap would be passed | `cap_test` | cap (midnight) |
| `TriageCapRefused` | `spend.SpendRefused` | `cap_global` | cap (midnight) |
| `TriageSpendCheck` | the reserve RPC errored | `spend_check` | infrastructure |
| `TriageTruncated` | `LlmBadOutput` whose message starts "Model response was cut off" | `truncated` | answer quality |
| `TriageBadAnswer` | any other `LlmBadOutput` (`LlmBadOutputPermanent` included) | `bad_answer` | answer quality |
| `TriageBusy` | `llm_gate.LlmBusy` (`llm_gate.py:72`, the gate wait ran out) | `busy` | infrastructure |
| `TriageTimeout` | the SDK timeout error `complete_json` lets through (verify the class at build) | `timeout` | infrastructure |
| `TriageModelError` | any other exception from the call | `model_error` | infrastructure |

  `_translate` is the only place that names the `llm` and `llm_gate` exception classes. Matching the cut-off case on the message text is fragile, so `tests/test_assistant_triage_model.py` pins it against the real messages raised at `llm.py:902` and `:969` and fails if they change. WI-7 imports only these names (and an `ALL` tuple) from the model module; the breaker opens on `TriageModelError`, `TriageTimeout` and `TriageBusy` (5.7).

- `triage_hold_ttl(settings) = llm_background_wait_seconds + assistant_triage_timeout_seconds x (third_party_llm_max_retries + 1) + 60` (330 s with the defaults), so the hold always covers the slowest call the lease allows.
- `max_out` scales with the batch (1200 + 200 per item, capped by the setting), so a small batch holds less and a full batch is never cut off by a low setting; the boot validator also requires `assistant_triage_max_output_tokens >= 1200 + 200 x assistant_triage_batch_max` (13). ### 5.5 Answer schema, validation and apply

Schema (strict, every property required, `additionalProperties false`):

```json
{"items": [{"key": "t1", "decision": "none|suggest|escalate",
            "reason": "<enum>", "action": "<enum or null>", "severity": "normal|high|urgent|null"}]}
```

`triage.validate(answer, batch_keys, kinds, role)` checks the answer's format only (owner question 6 (c): the model's choice is final; there is no fact predicate and no `REASON_PREDICATES` table):

- Keys must be the batch's keys; unknown keys dropped; a missing key returns that item to pending (one retry, then `skipped bad_answer`).
- `decision` must be in the enum; `reason` must be one of the reasons listed for that decision; `action` must be in the per-kind, per-role action catalog when `decision = suggest` and null otherwise; `severity` must be `normal`, `high` or `urgent` when `decision = escalate` (owner question 8) and null otherwise. Anything else downgrades the item to `none` with reason `invalid_answer` (logged as a count). (Guiding-principle call: a malformed answer is not a choice, so treating it as `none` does not second-guess the model.)
- A duplicate key keeps the first occurrence.
- Whatever the pack says, a well-formed choice stands: an escalation on a read bell, on a bid days away, or beside the router's own "Bid due" line is stored and emitted as chosen.

Reason enum (initial): `covered_by_app`, `already_seen`, `not_your_part`, `info_only`, `no_action_needed`, `due_soon_part_missing`, `due_soon_unread`, `waiting_on_you`, `unread_long`, `needs_decision`, `follow_up_due`, `changed_after_your_work`, `invalid_answer`. Escalate reasons: `due_soon_part_missing`, `due_soon_unread`, `waiting_on_you`, `unread_long`, `needs_decision`, `changed_after_your_work`. The reason is not a fact: it picks the escalation template and the Inbox label, and it is the audit record of why the model escalated (19.1 FQ5).

Action catalog (initial; per record kind, filtered by role so an Accountant never gets write work, P23): `open_receive_quotes`, `review_verify`, `review_scope_lines`, `open_call_in_list`, `review_changed_drawings`, `review_revision`, `review_approval_request`, `follow_up_vendor`, `create_followup_todo`, `ask_assistant_summary`. Each action maps to one English "Work on this" template with slots for the project number and Pacific times only, for example `review_changed_drawings`: "Help me check bid {number}: drawings changed {when}. Which of my open RFQs and quotes do the changed sheets affect?"

Apply, in one pass after validation:

- Every decision, `none`, `suggest` and `escalate` alike, goes to the database in one call, `repo.triage_finish(None, run_id, worker, now, "decided", None, items, usage)` (A6.3), together with the per-item skips of 5.3 step 3. For an `escalate` item the finish inserts the event in the same fenced transaction, with `project_id`, `subject_user_id`, `inbox_id` and the dedupe key taken from the item row and the payload `{inbox_id, triage_item_id, run_id, reason, severity_hint, source_kind, emitted_at}` (ids, enums and the finish's real-clock time only; `clean_payload` allowlist in `proactive.py`). The payload carries no template slot the router trusts: the router computes times, part wording and bell state itself (5.6). A crash before the finish leaves no event (the items return to pending through the lease retirement, without an attempt, and the next run pays for one more call, an accepted cost); a fenced-out finish emits nothing.

### 5.7 Failures

Three classes (CCF-5), one helper (A4, `assistant_triage_item_retry`):

- Infrastructure failures, no attempt counted: model error or refusal from the provider (`model_error`), `timeout`, `LlmBusy` (`busy`), spend check RPC error (`spend_check`, fail closed), `lease_expired`, and claims refused by the breaker. Items go back to pending with backoff (`min(60 x 2^infra_retries, 900)` seconds) and end only when they go stale: the inbox row leaves the window, or the bid's `internal_bid_at` passes (`skipped stale`). An outage, a Mac sleep or a `--reload` restart therefore never burns the time-critical rows.
- Answer-quality failures, attempts counted (3 at most): unparseable answer (`bad_answer`), a missing key, `truncated` (the batch's items are then retried one at a time, A6.2). Ladder 60 s, then 5 min; the third ends `skipped bad_answer` or `failed`.
- Cap refusals, nothing counted (owner question 13 (b)): `cap_global` (the company cap, B2) or `cap_test` (the live-test cap, 5.4). The batch's items go back to pending until the next Pacific midnight plus a per-item offset of up to 2 hours (A4) with `cap_deferred_at` set; after midnight an item whose inbox row is under 24 hours old is triaged, an older one settles `skipped cap` (A4 age-out rule). The process pauses claims for 15 minutes after a refusal (5.1 step 5). One log line per process per day for the first refusal.
- Breaker (per process, in `assistant_triage.py`): the first `model_error`, `timeout` or `busy` in a tick stops claiming for that tick and opens the breaker for 2 minutes, then 4, 8, up to 15 between probes (one claim allowed per probe; a success closes it). The admin health block (8.1) shows "paused: model unreachable" and the next probe time. `llm_health` has no breaker to reuse (`check()` is a status-bar probe only, `llm_health.py:268`).
- Unpriced model or non-OpenAI route (`TriageUnpriced`): `failed unpriced`, items back to pending as infrastructure (an operator fixes config); the health block shows it.
- Finish fence lost: nothing written, nothing emitted, log.

### 5.8 Both uvicorn workers

Both run the triage loop; correctness lives in Postgres: the enqueue is idempotent (unique item), the claim is per person under an advisory lock with `skip locked` and filtered by the caller's allowlist, leases run on the real clock, the finish is fenced and emits inside its own transaction, the event is deduped, and every triage hold is admitted under the same `hashtext('assistant_spend')` lock as every other assistant hold (B2), so the company cap holds across both workers. A person is never in two runs at once (the claim skips a person with a `claimed` run whose lease is live, and re-checks that under the per-person advisory lock before it inserts the run, A6.2 step 6). The fuse is checked and latched inside the claim, so both workers stop at the same batch boundary (A6.2 step 3). The breaker and the cap pause are per process, so each worker backs off on its own.

### 5.9 What triage reads

`assistant_inbox` (the person's rows, including the router's `bid.due_not_ready` rows for the `router_bid_due_row` fact), `assistant_triage_*`, `notifications` (state columns only: `read_at`, `dismissed_at` through `repo.notification_states`; `teams_sent_at`, `email_log_id` presence through the new `repo.notification_delivery_facts`; never `message`, question 3 (a)), `projects` (`_PROJECT_COLUMNS`: number, `current_stage`, `internal_bid_at`, `abandoned_at`, `failed_to_bid_at`), `rfqs` and the readiness tables through `due_digest.readiness_rows` and the shared `missing_quote_categories` (booleans only), the person's to-dos and pending approvals (counts), `assistant_profiles`, `assistant_settings`, `profiles`, and today's company triage spend (A6.7, only while the live-test cap is set). It reads no delegation. All through `repo.py` (assistant tables) or the existing read helpers.

### 14.1 Token assumptions (per call)

- Per item: about 180 input tokens (facts plus allowed lists), about 150 output tokens (the JSON line plus a reasoning allowance). The hold uses the larger `max_out` of 5.4 (1200 + 200 per item since P8L-3) because reasoning tokens bill as output. Measured on 2026-11-03 at the default effort: about 184 output tokens for one item (144 reasoning) and about 826 for nine (588 reasoning), so the 150 per item above is close for full batches and low for one item, whose reasoning varies (one live call passed 600).

- Midnight retry (P13): the worst realistic deferral is a day whose cap was reached early, deferring every later row. At the prod scenario that is at most about 300 rows, about 100 calls, about 0.05 USD of the new day's company budget, spread from 00:00 to 02:00 PT by the per-item offset (A4; MC-8), so about 50 calls an hour. Without the spread the drain took 10 to 20 minutes, and an escalation rate just over 10 percent on 300 retried rows would pass the hourly fuse wire (30) and pause triage for everyone until an admin flip in the morning; with it, the same rate gives about 15 escalations an hour. The retried rows still count in the hourly wire (19.1 FQ10).
- Pure sharing, no floor (P11): triage can take the company's last cent and chat can take triage's, whichever reserves first. At these prices triage cannot itself drain the pool; the realistic way triage stops is chat, AI Estimating page reads and browser tasks filling the 50 USD first. Browser holds count too (2.1 item 11, CCF-9): every active reservation is in `global_held`, and a queued browser task holds its stepped amount (1 USD steps up to 5 USD) for up to 8 hours plus run time. Worked case: three queued browser tasks grown to 5 USD each hold 15 USD; add 35 USD of settled chat and the company total reaches the 50 USD cap, so the next triage reserve is refused and that batch waits for midnight, with almost no triage spend involved. The admin block (8.1) shows `global_spent`, `global_held`, the browser share and the company triage spend so the cause is visible; 16.2 tests a refusal caused by an active `browser_task` hold.
- Live test and live eval: capped at 1 USD of company triage spend for the day by `ASSISTANT_TRIAGE_TEST_SPEND_CAP_USD` (13); the live test needs well under 0.01 USD of real calls, and the golden eval (about 40 cases, about 10 calls) about 0.01 USD.

## 15. Security

### 15.1 Threats and controls

| Threat | Control |
|---|---|
| Hostile text in a bell message, project name, vendor or GC name, note or email subject steers triage | The fact pack carries none of it (5.3, owner question 3 (a)), checked by `assert_pack_clean` and fuzzed in tests; the only strings are catalog enums, times, `t<n>` keys and pattern-checked project numbers. This is the main control now that nothing checks the model's choice and urgent is allowed: no planted text can reach the model, so none can cause an escalation or a night push |
| The model is talked into sending something to someone | Triage has no tool, no route, no token and no sender. Its only output is enums; the only side effect of `escalate` is one router event addressed to the row's owner, delivered by the router's own path and ledger. Recipients are never model-chosen |
| Escalation text carries a planted instruction or link | The text is a server template keyed by reason enum plus a project number and router-computed times; no model text reaches a bell, Teams card or email |
| A false claim reaches a person: the model picks a reason the facts do not support (for example "your part is still missing" when it is not) | Accepted by the owner for delivery (question 6 (c): the escalation is delivered). The text never asserts it: a factual clause appears only when the router computes it true now, otherwise the neutral variant is used (5.6, 19.1 FQ5) |
| False urgency: the model rings someone at night or on a weekend without cause | Accepted by the owner (question 8; owner note: "the model can send Teams at any hour with no fact checks"). Bounds: facts-only input, one escalation per row, the person's own mute and "not useful" marks (the third mark moves the kind to inbox only), the company ratio fuse (urgent counts), the prompt's urgent guidance, golden-set cases for night and weekend urgency (16.4), the health counts by severity |
| A stale worker, a switched-off company or a wrong item id produces an escalation | No separate emit: the escalation is inserted inside the fenced finish (A6.3), which requires the run's real-clock lease, the item still `claimed` under that run, the company switch on, the run claimed after the last switch-on and the fuse latch clear; subject, project and dedupe key come from the item row; the router checks the switch, the switch-on time (against the payload's real-clock `emitted_at`) and the latch again for this kind at route, delivery and Teams send time and withdraws what fails (5.6) |
| An assistant-run email logged as `system` | `send_mail(actor_kind="system")` raises inside an assistant context and forces `assistant_run_id` null (section 10) |
| Triage spends a person's budget | Every triage hold and call is under the company spend identity (A2a, P12): `spend.reserve` and `assistant_spend_reserve` refuse kind `triage` with any other id, and refuse the company id for any other kind; a CHECK on the reservations table pins the pairing (B1) |
| Escalation flooding (no per-person limit) | One escalation per record row (dedupe key `p8:esc:{inbox_id}`, inserted inside the finish, A6.3); only bell-sourced record rows; mute and auto-downgrade; quiet hours for normal and high; in-app only for held high escalations on non-workdays (P27); the company fuse (P10), latched in `assistant_settings` and checked inside the claim before every batch on both workers, so a storm stops within one batch per worker and stays stopped until an admin flip, and undelivered escalations are withdrawn while it is tripped |
| Loop: an escalation bell re-enters triage | Escalation bells are `assistant.inbox`, excluded by the `assistant.` prefix in `bell_recordable` (`proactive.py:504`); pinned |
| Spend abuse (a burst drains the pool) | Every call reserved under kind `triage` against the company cap (pure sharing, owner question 11 (b)); unpriced model or non-OpenAI route refused at boot and at call time; one call per batch, batches bounded per tick, one at a time; the breaker stops claims during an outage; a cap refusal pauses claims and defers rows to midnight; infrastructure retries never count as attempts but end when the row goes stale |
| Triage for a person who left | Per-batch eligibility (active, internal, assistant on, 5.3); offboarding hook (11); the router's own eligibility at route and delivery time; send-time recheck. No delegation is involved (owner question 15 (c)) |
| Writes without a click | Triage has no write path at all; "Work on this" prefills only |
| Inbox tool leaks or enables writes | Read only, own rows only, route answers only a `bdra_` principal, results tainted (sticky conversation taint, binding strips matching text from later writes) |
| Cross-user data | Batches are per person; fact queries are scoped by `user_id`; tests pin that a batch never holds two people's rows |
| Model output with extra fields or ids | Strict schema plus `validate`: unknown keys dropped, wrong enums downgraded to `none` (a format check) |

### 15.2 Taint rules applied

- Triage input is untainted by construction (question 3 (a)). Its output is enums, so there is nothing to bind.
- `inbox_list` results are always tainted (P16); the conversation becomes tainted for life (`docs/ASSISTANT.md:100`), so later writes in that chat need fresh cards with binding against the stored corpus (`repo.tainted_results`, `repo.py:835`).
- "Work on this" text is a server template without URLs or recipients; it is typed into the composer, so it becomes a user message; binding treats it as user text, which is safe because it holds no value that came from a tainted source.

### 15.3 What triage must never be able to do (pinned by tests)

Call any sender; import the loop, executor, jobs or prompts; reach a route through the shim; write a BDR table; write another person's assistant row; emit more than one escalation per row; emit from a fenced-out run, from a run claimed before the last switch-on, while the company switch is off or while the fuse is latched; reserve or log under a person's id; run without a priced OpenAI route; run in the interactive gate tier; read a bell message or any free text.
